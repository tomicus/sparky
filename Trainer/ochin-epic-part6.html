📅 6.1 Scheduling Fundamentals in Ochin Epic

Scheduling is the front door of your clinic. Every patient interaction begins with an appointment, and how that appointment is created, tracked, and closed directly affects clinical care, quality metrics, and revenue. In Ochin's shared Epic instance, scheduling is standardized across all member sites — but configured per clinic.

Ochin Scheduling Model: Ochin manages the Epic scheduling build — appointment types, slot durations, and template structures are configured by Ochin analysts in partnership with each clinic. You schedule within these pre-built templates. If your templates don't match your clinic's needs, submit a request through my.ochin.org — don't try to work around the template.

Navigating to the Schedule in Epic

Epic Hyperspace — Scheduling

Scheduling a New Appointment — Step by Step

  1. Search for the patient first
    Always search before scheduling — confirm the patient exists in Ochin's shared database. Never create a new patient record without thoroughly searching. Use name + DOB, or MRN.
  2. Select "Schedule Appointment" from the patient chart
    This pre-populates the patient's name and demographic information into the scheduling dialog.
  3. Select the Appointment Type
    Choose from your clinic's configured appointment types (e.g., "New Patient — Primary Care," "Established Patient — 30 min," "Well Child — 2 Year"). The type determines the slot duration and provider template it can fill.
  4. Select the Provider and Department
    Choose the provider the patient wants to see and your clinic department. Epic will only show time slots that match the appointment type's template configuration for that provider.
  5. Use "Find Opening" to locate available slots
    Click Find Opening to search for the next available appointment that matches your criteria. Filter by date range, provider, and appointment type. Results show available slots in chronological order.
  6. Select the slot and confirm details
    Click the desired slot. Confirm the date, time, provider, and department. Add any visit reason, interpreter need, or special instructions.
  7. Document the Visit Reason
    Enter a brief reason for the visit in the "Chief Complaint" or "Visit Reason" field. This helps the clinical team prepare and is used for UDS documentation.
  8. Accept and confirm the appointment
    Click Accept. Epic saves the appointment, updates the provider schedule, and — if configured — triggers a MyChart notification or automated reminder to the patient.

Appointment Reminders & Patient Communication

Ochin's Epic is configured to send automated appointment reminders via the patient's preferred communication channel. This is a key tool for reducing no-show rates — which directly impacts FQHC productivity and UDS access measures.

🗓️ 6.2 Appointment Types & Schedule Templates

Every appointment in Epic is assigned an Appointment Type that defines its duration, purpose, and which provider template slots it can fill. Understanding appointment types is essential for schedulers — using the wrong type creates billing errors, template mismatches, and gaps in quality reporting.

⚠️ Appointment Type Matters for Billing: The appointment type feeds into the billing workflow. Using the wrong type — for example, scheduling a Medicare Annual Wellness Visit as a "Follow-Up" — can result in incorrect claim submission and denied payment. When in doubt, ask your billing team which appointment type to use.

Understanding Schedule Templates

A schedule template is the pre-built pattern of appointment slots for each provider and day — it defines which appointment types can be scheduled in which slots, how many slots exist per day, and what the hold or block rules are.

💡 Adding a Same-Day Block: If a provider is out unexpectedly, schedulers can add a block to their schedule for that day to prevent new bookings. Navigate to the provider's schedule → click the time period → select "Add Block." This does NOT cancel existing appointments — those must be rescheduled manually or via mass reschedule (contact Ochin support for mass reschedule tools).

🚶 6.3 No-Shows, Cancellations & the Waitlist

Accurately documenting appointment outcomes — whether a patient showed up, cancelled, or didn't come at all — is essential for UDS reporting, access metrics, and identifying patients who need outreach. Using the wrong appointment disposition is a data quality problem that affects your clinic's performance measures.

Appointment Disposition — Use the Right Code

DispositionWhen to Use ItUDS Impact
CompletedPatient arrived, was seen by provider, encounter is complete and signedCounts as a patient visit for UDS Table 5
ArrivedPatient has checked in but has not yet been seen — interim status during visitNot counted until completed
Cancelled — PatientPatient called ahead of time to cancel their appointmentDoes NOT count as a visit; does not penalize UDS access metric
Cancelled — ClinicClinic cancelled the appointment (provider out, emergency closure)Does NOT count as a visit; tracked for access metric review
No-ShowPatient did not arrive AND did not call to cancel in advanceCounted in no-show rate — a key access quality measure
Left Without Being SeenPatient arrived and checked in but left before being seen by a providerTracked separately — signals wait time or access issues
⚠️ Never mark a No-Show as Cancelled: This distorts your clinic's no-show rate and access metrics, which affect UDS reporting and can impact HRSA grant assessments. If the patient didn't call, didn't come — it's a No-Show.

Processing a No-Show in Epic

  1. At the end of the appointment time (or end of day), review the schedule for patients who are still in "Scheduled" or "Arrived" status but were never seen
  2. Click the appointment in the schedule view → select No Show from the disposition options
  3. Add a note if relevant — did you attempt to contact the patient? Document it: "Called patient at 2:14 PM — no answer, left voicemail."
  4. Consider same-day outreach — for high-risk patients (diabetics with poor control, prenatal patients, patients due for cancer screening), a same-day no-show call can recover the visit or prompt immediate rescheduling
  5. Flag for follow-up if the patient has had multiple no-shows — a pattern of no-shows may indicate transportation barriers, work schedule conflicts, or care disengagement that your care team should address proactively

Managing Cancellations

When a patient calls to cancel, document it correctly and offer to reschedule immediately — do not just cancel and end the interaction.

  1. Pull up the patient's appointment → click Cancel Appointment
  2. Select the cancellation reason — Epic prompts for a reason: "Patient request," "Transportation," "Work conflict," "Feeling better," etc. These reasons are tracked and help identify systemic barriers.
  3. Offer to reschedule immediately — ask the patient when they'd like to come back and book the next appointment before ending the call. A cancelled appointment with an immediate reschedule is a retained patient.
  4. For provider-initiated cancellations — contact all affected patients promptly. Epic's mass messaging tools can help notify multiple patients at once. Document all contact attempts.

The Waitlist — Managing Demand for Scarce Slots

When no appointments are available in a provider's schedule, patients should be added to the Waitlist rather than told to "call back later."

  1. From the scheduling dialog, when no slots are available, click Add to Waitlist
  2. Document the patient's preferences: preferred provider, appointment type, urgency, date range availability, and best contact number
  3. When a slot opens (due to a cancellation or new template capacity), schedulers review the waitlist and contact patients in priority order
  4. Schedule the patient from the waitlist — use the "Schedule from Waitlist" function to book them directly without re-entering their information
  5. Remove resolved entries — once a patient is scheduled or has found care elsewhere, remove them from the waitlist to keep it accurate
💡 Waitlist Best Practice: Review your clinic's waitlist every morning. Yesterday's cancellations = today's open slots. A proactive scheduler can fill most cancellation gaps from the waitlist before the open slot is even noticed.

💻 6.4 Telehealth Scheduling & Visit Workflow in Ochin Epic

Telehealth has become a permanent part of FQHC care delivery. Ochin's Epic supports video visits through MyChart Video Visits and integrated telehealth platforms. The scheduling workflow is similar to in-person visits, but there are critical differences in how the visit is prepared, conducted, and billed.

Ochin Telehealth Platform: Ochin member clinics typically use MyChart Video Visits (built into Epic/MyChart) or a third-party platform integrated with Epic (such as Doximity or Zoom for Healthcare). Your clinic's Ochin analyst will tell you which platform is active and how it connects to the encounter. The billing and documentation workflow is the same regardless of platform.

Scheduling a Telehealth Appointment

  1. Select the Telehealth appointment type
    Use the specific telehealth appointment type — e.g., "Video Visit — Established Patient." Do NOT use a standard in-person type and add a note — the appointment type determines billing configuration.
  2. Confirm the patient has a MyChart account
    MyChart Video Visits require an active MyChart account. If the patient doesn't have one, help them enroll or use an alternative telehealth method.
  3. Confirm technology access
    Ask: "Do you have a smartphone, tablet, or computer with a camera?" Document if patient has technology barriers — your clinic may have an alternate audio-only visit option.
  4. Send patient instructions
    Epic can send automated MyChart instructions for the video visit — test link, join instructions, and what to have ready (medication list, blood pressure cuff if available).
  5. Collect consent for telehealth
    Telehealth consent must be documented at or before the visit. Ochin's Epic has a configured telehealth consent that can be sent via MyChart or collected verbally and documented at check-in.

Day-of-Visit: Telehealth Check-In Workflow

  1. Check the patient into the encounter — same as in-person. Use the Appointment Desk or check-in workflow. The encounter must be open to bill.
  2. Confirm patient location at time of visit
    This is legally required for telehealth billing. Ask: "Where are you located right now?" Document the patient's state — they must be in a state where your provider is licensed. This is a compliance requirement, not a formality.
  3. Confirm and document telehealth consent if not already done via MyChart
  4. Document the visit modality
    In the encounter, document that the visit was conducted via telehealth and whether it was video (audio + visual) or audio-only. This determines which billing codes apply.
  5. Provider joins the video visit through Epic/MyChart or the integrated platform
  6. Complete the visit note normally — same SOAP note structure, same order entry, same sign-off process as in-person
  7. Disposition the appointment as "Completed" just like in-person — the telehealth visit generates a billable encounter

Audio-Only Visits — When Video Isn't Possible

Some patients cannot access video technology. Audio-only (telephone) visits may be permitted for certain visit types under CMS and state Medicaid rules — but they have different billing codes and restrictions.

Visit TypeCPT/HCPCS CodeNotes
Telehealth — Video (synchronous, audio + video)Standard E&M codes (99213, 99214, etc.) with GT modifierFully reimbursed by Medicare and most Medicaid programs
Audio-Only Telephone Visit99441–99443 (by complexity/time)Lower reimbursement; some insurers don't cover; document reason patient couldn't use video

⚠️ Telehealth Billing Rules Change Frequently: CMS telehealth waivers and state Medicaid telehealth policies have changed significantly since COVID. Always confirm current billing rules with your Ochin billing team before assuming a telehealth visit type is covered. What was covered last year may not be covered today.

🏥 6.5 FQHC Billing & the Prospective Payment System (PPS)

FQHC billing is fundamentally different from standard fee-for-service billing. Understanding how Federally Qualified Health Centers are reimbursed — and how Epic captures the data needed to generate that reimbursement — is critical for anyone involved in clinical documentation or billing at an Ochin site.

What Makes a "Qualifying FQHC Visit"?

Not every encounter generates a PPS payment. A qualifying FQHC visit requires ALL of the following:

  • The patient must be seen face-to-face (or via qualifying telehealth) by a billable FQHC provider — physician, NP, PA, CNM, or behavioral health clinician
  • The visit must occur at an HRSA-designated FQHC service site — not off-site unless the site has special designation
  • A signed encounter note must be in Epic documenting the visit
  • At least one diagnosis (ICD-10) must be assigned
  • The visit must be billed with the appropriate FQHC encounter code (T1015 for Medicaid; PPS-qualifying visit code for Medicare)
⚠️ Unsigned Notes = No Bill: If a provider sees a patient but never signs the note in Epic, the encounter cannot be billed. Unsigned notes are the single most common reason for lost FQHC revenue. Ochin billing teams run daily reports on unsigned notes and escalate to providers — but the best solution is signing the note before you leave.

Same-Day Visits — FQHC Rules

FQHCs can bill for more than one visit on the same day in limited circumstances. This is important at integrated clinics where a patient sees both a primary care provider AND a behavioral health clinician on the same day.

  • Primary Care + Behavioral Health on same day: Two qualifying encounters CAN be billed — one for each provider, each with their own signed note and diagnosis codes
  • Two primary care visits same day: Generally only one is billable unless the second is for a distinctly different presenting problem and is documented separately
  • Dental + Medical on same day: If your FQHC has dental services, dental and medical visits on the same day are separate qualifying encounters
✓ Ochin Integration Advantage: When a primary care provider and behavioral health clinician both see a patient on the same day, Ochin's Epic is configured to create two separate encounters — each with their own note and billing. The billing team reviews these daily. Make sure both providers sign their notes before end of day to capture both encounter payments.

🧾 6.6 Encounter Coding, ICD-10, & E&M Levels in Ochin Epic

Clinical documentation drives billing codes. In Ochin's Epic, the coding workflow is semi-automated — the system proposes codes based on your documentation, but providers and coders must review and confirm accuracy. Accurate coding protects your clinic from audits and ensures maximum appropriate reimbursement.

The Coding Workflow in Epic — Charge Capture

  1. Provider completes the visit note
    The note's content — History, Exam, Medical Decision Making or time — determines the E&M level. Document thoroughly and specifically.
  2. Navigate to the Diagnoses & Orders section (or the Coding tab, depending on your clinic's Epic build)
    Epic displays the diagnosis list associated with the encounter. Every diagnosis should have an ICD-10 code.
  3. Assign visit diagnoses
    Add all diagnoses addressed at this visit — not just the primary complaint. If you managed diabetes AND hypertension AND ordered a lipid panel, all three should appear in the encounter diagnoses.
  4. Review the proposed E&M level
    Epic's coding engine proposes an E&M level based on your documentation (99213, 99214, etc.). Review and confirm — if your documentation supports a higher level but the system proposes lower, check whether your note is complete enough to justify the higher code.
  5. Sign the note
    Signing releases the encounter for billing review. Unsigned = unbilled. The billing team cannot touch an unsigned encounter.
  6. Billing team reviews and submits
    Ochin billing staff review the encounter, confirm codes, apply any necessary modifiers, and submit the claim to the payer.

ICD-10 codes are the diagnostic foundation of every claim. Vague or incomplete diagnosis coding leads to claim denials, audit risk, and inaccurate UDS quality reporting.

💰 6.7 Copays, Collections & the Charge Review Workflow

Revenue cycle management at an FQHC involves collecting copays at the point of service, reviewing charges before they are submitted, and managing patient balances — all while applying the Sliding Fee Scale appropriately for uninsured and underinsured patients.

Collecting Copays at Check-In

  1. During check-in, review the patient's insurance and expected copay
    Epic displays the patient's active coverage and the expected copay from the insurance eligibility data. If eligibility was verified, the expected copay should be accurate.
  2. Inform the patient of their expected copay
    Tell the patient their copay before the visit — surprises at checkout reduce collections. "Your copay today is $20 — would you like to take care of that now?"
  3. Use Epic's "Pay" function to post the payment
    In the check-in screen → click Pay → select Payment Type (Cash, Check, Credit Card) → enter amount → Save. Epic posts the payment to the patient account and generates a receipt.
  4. Print a receipt if the patient requests one — click "Print Receipt" after posting
  5. For patients on the Sliding Fee Scale
    Their adjusted copay is already reflected in Epic based on their SFS tier. Do not collect more than the SFS-adjusted amount from sliding fee patients.

Patient Balances & Payment Plans

Patients at FQHCs often carry balances that must be managed sensitively. Ochin's Epic has tools for managing balances without creating barriers to care.

  • Balance review at check-in: Epic shows any outstanding balance when the patient checks in. Staff should ask if the patient would like to make a payment — but should never deny care based on an unpaid balance (HRSA requirement)
  • Payment plans: For larger balances, Epic can establish a monthly payment plan. Navigate to the patient account → Payment Plan → set the monthly amount and duration
  • Write-offs: If a balance is uncollectable or the patient qualifies for additional financial assistance, billing staff can apply a write-off. This requires supervisor authorization per your clinic's policy
  • Never deny care for non-payment: FQHCs are prohibited by HRSA from refusing care due to inability to pay. If a patient cannot pay, document it and proceed with care — address the balance separately

The Charge Review Process

Before a claim is submitted to a payer, it goes through a Charge Review process in Epic. This is where billing staff review the encounter, confirm codes, and ensure the claim is complete and accurate.

  1. Provider signs the note
    This triggers the encounter to move to the billing queue for review.
  2. Billing staff open the encounter in Charge Review
    Navigate to Billing → Charge Review. The encounter appears with the proposed charges based on the visit documentation.
  3. Review diagnosis codes
    Confirm all diagnoses have specific ICD-10 codes and that the primary diagnosis is listed first. The primary diagnosis drives the claim.
  4. Review the E&M level
    Confirm the proposed CPT code matches the documentation. If the level seems too low or too high for what was documented, flag for provider clarification.
  5. Apply FQHC wrapping
    For Medicare and Medicaid, Ochin's Epic is configured to automatically "wrap" the charges into the FQHC encounter structure. Billing staff confirm the wrap is applied correctly.
  6. Submit the claim
    After review, click Accept → the claim is queued for electronic submission to the payer. Ochin's billing engine handles claim transmission.
⏰
Timely Filing Deadlines
Insurance payers have strict deadlines for claim submission — typically 90 days to 12 months from the date of service, depending on the payer. Medicare requires claims within 12 months. Some commercial plans require 90 days. Unsigned notes delay billing and can cause missed filing deadlines — resulting in denied claims that cannot be appealed. Sign your notes the same day as the visit.

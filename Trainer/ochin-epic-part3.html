📂 3.1 Navigating the Patient Chart

The patient chart in Ochin Epic is the central workspace for all clinical activity. Once you open a patient's chart, you'll see a structured layout designed for fast access to the most critical information.

Opening a Patient Chart

  • From the Schedule: Click or double-click the patient's appointment
  • From Patient Search (Ctrl+E): Find the patient → click their name to open chart
  • From In Basket: Click a message related to a patient — their chart opens alongside the message
  • Recent Patients list: Epic Button → Recent Patients shows your last 10 opened charts

Chart Layout — Activity Tabs

Once inside a chart, the top row of tabs changes based on the patient's context. Common tabs for clinic visits:

The Patient Header — Always Visible

The patient header bar at the top of the chart shows critical safety information at all times:

  • Allergies — displayed in red. "NKA" = No Known Allergies. "NKDA" = No Known Drug Allergies
  • Medications — count of active medications; click to jump to med list
  • Active Problems — count and quick preview
  • Alerts / Flags — safety alerts, care team flags, interpreter needed, etc.
  • Pregnancy status — if documented, shows EDD and gestational age
⚠️ Always check the patient header FIRST when opening any chart. Allergy alerts in red require your attention before ordering anything. A patient with a Penicillin allergy receiving Amoxicillin is a preventable harm — Epic will alert you, but you must be aware.

The Chart Navigator (Left Panel)

When writing a note or working through a visit, the Navigator appears on the left side of the chart. It's a structured outline of the visit sections. Click any section to jump directly to it. Sections with a green checkmark are complete; gray means incomplete.

💡 Tip — Sticky Note / Patient Flag: You can add a sticky note to a patient's chart (visible to all staff) for important alerts: "Always needs interpreter," "Escalate billing questions to supervisor," "Preferred name: Alex." To add: click the flag icon in the patient header. Sticky notes are not part of the medical record — they are internal workflow notes.

📝 3.2 Writing Visit Notes in Ochin Epic

Clinical notes in Ochin Epic are written directly in the chart using the Note Editor. Notes are structured, searchable, and become part of the permanent legal medical record. Ochin Epic uses the standard Epic note architecture with FQHC-specific templates pre-built by the Ochin team.

Starting a New Note

  1. Open the patient's chart and navigate to the current encounter
    From the schedule, click on the appointment. The encounter opens automatically. Or: Chart tab → New Note button (or Ctrl+Shift+N).
  2. Select the Note Type
    A dialog asks which type of note to create. Common types at Ochin FQHCs:
    • Office Visit Note — standard outpatient visit (most common)
    • Telephone Encounter Note — documents phone calls with patients
    • Nurse Triage Note — MA/RN triage documentation
    • Behavioral Health Note — mental health/SUD visits (may have different templates)
    • Preventive Care Note — annual wellness visits
    • Procedure Note — in-office procedures (IUD, colposcopy, etc.)
  3. Select a Template (SmartForm or Blank)
    Ochin has pre-built templates for common visit types. Select the appropriate template — it pre-populates section headers and pulls in relevant patient data automatically.
  4. The Note Editor opens
    You'll see the structured SOAP layout with the Navigator on the left. Work through each section top to bottom.

SOAP Note Structure in Ochin Epic

Ochin Epic organizes clinical notes in the standard SOAP format, though the navigator labels may vary slightly by note type:

CHIEF COMPLAINT: Patient presents for follow-up of type 2 diabetes and annual wellness visit. SUBJECTIVE (History of Present Illness): Jane Doe is a 41-year-old female with a history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia presenting for routine follow-up. She reports her blood sugars have been running 130-160 fasting at home. She denies polyuria, polydipsia, or hypoglycemic episodes. She has been adherent to metformin 1000mg BID and reports no GI side effects. REVIEW OF SYSTEMS: *** SmartLink auto-pulls from ROS flowsheet *** Constitutional: Denies fever, chills, weight loss. Cardiovascular: Denies chest pain, palpitations. Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath, cough. OBJECTIVE: Vitals: {VITALS SMARTLINK — auto-populates from flowsheet} Physical Exam: General: Alert and oriented, NAD, well-appearing. HEENT: Normocephalic, atraumatic. PERRL. Oropharynx clear. CV: RRR, no murmurs. Pulm: CTAB, no wheeze or crackles. Abdomen: Soft, NT/ND, no HSM. Extremities: No edema. Bilateral DP pulses intact. Neuro: A&O x3, no focal deficits. ASSESSMENT & PLAN: 1. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (E11.9) — uncontrolled - Last A1c 7.8% (3 months ago). Order repeat A1c today. - Continue Metformin 1000mg BID. - Referral to diabetes education placed. - Patient counseled on diet and foot care. 2. Hypertension (I10) — controlled - BP 128/76 today. Continue Lisinopril 10mg daily. 3. Preventive Care - Flu vaccine administered today (documented in immunizations). - Mammogram due — order placed. Time spent: 25 minutes total, >50% counseling. Electronically signed by: {PROVIDER.NAME}, {DEGREE} — {DATE}

Pulling Data Into Notes with SmartLinks

SmartLinks automatically pull live patient data into your note text. They appear as tokens that resolve when the note is signed. Common examples:

SmartLink TokenWhat It Pulls In
@VITALS@Today's vitals from the flowsheet
@MEDLIST@Current active medication list
@PROBLEMLIST@Active problem list
@ALLERGIES@Documented allergies with reactions
@LABRESULTS@Most recent lab results for selected tests
@AGE@ / @DOB@Patient age / date of birth

Telephone Encounter Notes

When you call or receive a call from a patient, document it as a Telephone Encounter in Epic — not as a visit note. From the patient's chart: New → Telephone Encounter. Document:

  • Date and time of call
  • Who was called (patient, guardian, pharmacy)
  • Reason for call
  • Information given / instructions provided
  • Patient's response and plan

Telephone encounters are billable in some circumstances and create a medico-legal record of communication. Never rely on memory — document every patient call.

🔑 Documentation Is the Legal Record: In a malpractice case, the standard rule is: "If it wasn't documented, it didn't happen." Every clinical interaction — including phone calls, patient education, and care coordination — must be documented in Epic. Completeness protects both the patient and the provider.

✨ 3.3 SmartPhrases & Dot Phrases

SmartPhrases (also called Dot Phrases) are text shortcuts that expand into full paragraphs, templates, or data pulls when typed in the Epic note editor. They are one of the most powerful time-saving tools in Epic and dramatically reduce documentation time once mastered.

How to Use a Dot Phrase

  1. Type a period (.) followed by the phrase abbreviation in any Epic text field while in the note editor. Example: type .hpi
  2. Press F2 or Enter to expand the phrase. The full template text populates instantly.
  3. Fill in the wildcards — placeholder text appears between *** markers. Press F2 to jump between wildcards and fill them in or delete them.
  4. Edit the expanded text as needed to reflect the specific patient encounter. SmartPhrases are starting templates — personalize them for accuracy.

Creating Your Own SmartPhrases

Providers and clinical staff can create personal SmartPhrases for their own use. Ochin analysts also maintain shared clinic-wide phrases.

  1. Go to: Epic Button → My SmartPhrases (or search "SmartPhrase" in the Epic Button menu)
  2. Click "New" to create a new phrase. Give it a short, memorable abbreviation — your phrases are prefixed with your initials by convention (e.g., .jsdiabetes for Dr. J. Smith's diabetes template).
  3. Type the expanded text. Use *** as wildcard placeholders for parts that change per patient. Insert SmartLinks using the Insert SmartLink button to auto-pull patient data.
  4. Save and test in a note. Type your abbreviation and press F2 to confirm it expands correctly.
SmartPhrase Editor — .mydiabetes
Abbreviation: .mydiabetes Author: Smith, John MD DIABETES FOLLOW-UP NOTE: Patient is a ***age*** year old with type 2 diabetes. Last A1c: ***result & date***. Goal <7%. Home glucose log reviewed: ***findings***. Current medications: @MEDLIST@ Adherence: ***good / fair / poor***. Side effects: ***none / describe***. Diabetes complications screening: - Foot exam today: ***normal / abnormal findings*** - Last eye exam: ***date***. Referral needed: ***yes/no*** - Urine microalbumin: ***date & result*** - Nephrology referral: ***not indicated / placed*** Patient education provided on: ***topics***. Return to clinic in ***timeframe***.
💡 Tip — SmartList Dropdowns: Inside a SmartPhrase, you can embed SmartLists — clickable dropdown menus with pre-set choices. Example: instead of typing "good / fair / poor," a SmartList presents those three options as clickable buttons, and your selection inserts the right word automatically. Ask your Ochin super-user to help you add SmartLists to your personal templates.
⚠️ SmartPhrase Risk — Copy/Paste Errors: A common documentation error is expanding a SmartPhrase template and forgetting to fill in the wildcards, leaving placeholder text like "***describe***" in a signed note. Always read through your note before signing to ensure all wildcards are replaced with real patient-specific content.

📊 3.4 Flowsheets & Vitals Entry

Flowsheets are structured data-entry grids in Epic used to record vitals, rooming information, preventive health screenings, and nursing assessments. Unlike free-text notes, flowsheet data is stored as discrete, measurable values — enabling reports, trend graphs, and quality measure tracking.

Entering Vitals (MA / Nursing Workflow)

  1. Open the patient's encounter and navigate to the Flowsheets section in the navigator, or click the Vitals tab.
  2. Select the correct flowsheet row for each vital sign. Common vitals rows in Ochin Epic:

    Weight (kg/lbs) Height (in/cm) BMI (auto-calculated) BP Systolic BP Diastolic Pulse Temp (°F/°C) O2 Sat (%) Respirations Pain Score (0-10)
  3. Enter values in each cell and press Tab to move to the next. Values outside normal reference ranges will be highlighted in red automatically.
  4. Note the BP position and site if clinically relevant (e.g., "Left arm, seated"). Some Ochin flowsheets have a BP Comments row for this.
  5. Click Accept / Sign Flowsheet to save. Unsigned flowsheet data does not appear in the provider's note SmartLinks until accepted.

Rooming Flowsheets (MA / Nursing)

Beyond vitals, Ochin Epic includes structured flowsheets for the full rooming process:

FlowsheetWhat to Document

Allergies ReviewConfirm current allergies with patient; update if changed. Mark as "Reviewed."

🔑 PHQ-9 in Ochin Epic: The PHQ-9 depression screening is a required quality measure for most Ochin member clinics. When you enter the PHQ-9 score in the flowsheet, Ochin Epic can automatically:
  • Calculate the severity level (Minimal / Mild / Moderate / Severe)
  • Trigger a Best Practice Advisory (BPA) if the score is ≥10
  • Prompt for a safety assessment if question 9 (suicidal ideation) is positive
  • Auto-populate the score into the provider's note via SmartLink
Always document the PHQ-9 as discrete data in the flowsheet — not just as free text in the note.

Viewing Flowsheet Trends

Epic's Flowsheet Trend View shows values over time in a table or graph. This is invaluable for chronic disease management. To access: open the Flowsheet → click the Trend button or toggle to graph view. You can see a patient's blood pressure, weight, A1c, and other values plotted over months or years.

🩺 3.5 Problem List & Visit Diagnoses

The Active Problem List

The Problem List is one of the most important sections of the patient's chart. It is a longitudinal list of the patient's active, chronic, and significant medical conditions. It is not a visit-by-visit diagnosis list — it persists across encounters and is visible to every provider who opens the chart.

Adding a Problem to the Problem List

  1. From Chart → Problem List or the navigator section within a note: click Add.
  2. Search for the diagnosis by name or ICD-10 code. Type "diabetes" and a list of options appears. Select the most specific code available:
    E11.9 — Type 2 DM without complications E11.65 — Type 2 DM with hyperglycemia E11.40 — Type 2 DM with diabetic neuropathy
  3. Set the Status: Active (ongoing), Resolved (no longer present), or Deleted (entered in error). Most new additions are Active.
  4. Set the Date of Onset if known. This date helps track chronicity for quality reporting and coding.
  5. Click Accept. The problem now appears on the Problem List across all future encounters.
💡 Specificity Matters: Use the most specific ICD-10 code available. "Diabetes mellitus" (E11.9) is acceptable but "Type 2 DM with diabetic chronic kidney disease Stage 3" (E11.65 + N18.3) is more accurate, better reflects the patient's complexity, supports higher-level coding, and improves quality measure reporting.

Visit Diagnoses vs. Problem List

Problem ListVisit / Encounter Diagnoses
PurposeLongitudinal record of all conditionsDiagnoses addressed at THIS visit
Persists?Yes — carries forward to all encountersNo — specific to this encounter only
Used for billing?Not directlyYes — drives the claim for this visit

Adding Visit Diagnoses (for Billing)

At the end of each visit, the provider (or MA under supervision) must enter the Visit Diagnoses in the Diagnosis section of the encounter. These flow to the claim.

  • Navigate to Chart → Diagnoses or the Diagnosis section in the navigator
  • Click Add from Problem List to pull in existing problems addressed today — this is the fastest method
  • Or Search and Add a new diagnosis not yet on the problem list
  • Mark each diagnosis as: Primary (main reason for visit), Secondary, or Additional
  • The Primary Diagnosis must align with the Chief Complaint and the majority of the visit — this drives E/M coding level and claim adjudication
⚠️ Coding Tip: The primary diagnosis on the claim must be specific enough to support medical necessity for any orders placed. If you order an A1c, the primary diagnosis should be a diabetes code — not "fatigue" or "wellness visit." Mismatched diagnoses cause claim denials and may trigger compliance reviews.

Resolving and Managing Problems

  • When a condition is no longer active: click the problem → Edit → change Status to Resolved. It moves to the Resolved Problems section but remains in the chart history.
  • If a problem was added in error: Status → Deleted. Never delete a real problem — use Resolved instead.
  • Review and clean up the problem list at every visit. An accurate, current problem list is a key patient safety tool.

🔏 3.6 Signing Notes & Cosigning in Ochin Epic

Every clinical note, order, and documentation entry in Epic must be signed to become part of the legal medical record. Ochin Epic enforces signature rules based on your role and licensure — and has a built-in cosignature workflow for supervised providers, which is especially important in FQHC settings.

Signing Your Own Note (Providers)

  1. Review the complete note — read through every section, confirm SmartLink data populated correctly, ensure no wildcard placeholders (***) remain.
  2. Click "Sign" at the bottom of the note editor or use the keyboard shortcut Ctrl+Shift+S. A confirmation dialog may appear.
  3. The note is now locked and signed. It appears in the chart with your name, credentials, and the date/time of signing. It becomes part of the permanent medical record.
  4. Addendum if corrections are needed: You cannot edit a signed note — you must add an Addendum. Click the note → Addendum → type your correction → sign the addendum. The original note and addendum both remain in the chart.
⚠️ Never sign a note you didn't write or review. Your signature is your legal attestation that the content is accurate and reflects your clinical judgment. Signing another provider's note without reviewing it is a compliance and liability risk.

Cosignature Workflows in Ochin (FQHC-Specific)

FQHCs have specific supervision requirements. Ochin Epic enforces these through cosignature rules:

Provider TypeCosign Required ByNotes
Medical StudentAttending Physician (MD/DO)Student signs first; attending reviews and cosigns. Attending's cosignature makes it billable.
Resident / FellowAttending PhysicianDepends on program supervision requirements. Often required for all notes.
Nurse Practitioner (NP)Collaborating MD (if required by state)Varies by state. Many states allow independent NP practice. Check your state and clinic policy.
Physician Assistant (PA)Supervising PhysicianPA signs first; supervising MD cosigns within the required timeframe (often 24–72 hrs).
Registered Nurse (RN)Ordering Physician (for telephone orders)Verbal/telephone orders must be cosigned by the ordering provider within 24 hours.
Medical Assistant (MA)Supervising RN or ProviderMAs document vitals and rooming flowsheets; these are typically accepted rather than signed with cosign workflow.

How Cosigning Works in Epic

  1. The supervised provider writes and signs the note first. Upon signing, Epic automatically routes the note to the supervising provider's In Basket for cosignature.
  2. The supervisor opens In Basket → Cosign Messages. They see a list of notes awaiting their cosignature.
  3. The supervisor opens each note, reviews it thoroughly, and either: Cosign (approve as written) or Edit & Cosign (make corrections, then cosign). If there are errors, the supervisor can route the note back to the writer with comments.
  4. After cosignature, the note is complete and reflects both signatures. The note shows: "Signed by: J. Resident, MD — Cosigned by: A. Attending, MD."

Cosign Deadlines and Escalation

  • FQHC cosignature timeframes vary — commonly 24 hours for routine notes, immediate for verbal/telephone orders
  • Epic has built-in escalation: if a cosignature is not completed within the configured timeframe, a reminder appears in the supervisor's In Basket and may escalate to a department administrator
  • Unsigned notes and pending cosignatures are tracked in quality and compliance reports — your clinic's compliance team monitors these
💡 Tip — Signing Multiple Notes at Once: In your In Basket, you can sign or cosign multiple notes without opening each one individually. From the In Basket message list, select multiple messages → right-click → Sign All. Use this cautiously — you should still review each note before signing.

📤 3.7 After Visit Summary (AVS) & Visit Wrap-Up

The After Visit Summary (AVS) is a patient-facing document generated at the end of every clinic visit. It is a Meaningful Use / Promoting Interoperability requirement — patients must be offered the AVS at every visit. Ochin Epic generates it automatically from the visit documentation.

What the AVS Contains

  • Visit date, provider, and clinic contact information
  • Today's diagnoses and chief complaint
  • Instructions and follow-up plan from the Assessment & Plan
  • Medications — current list with any new prescriptions or changes highlighted
  • Orders placed today — labs, imaging, referrals
  • Upcoming appointments
  • Health education handouts specific to the patient's conditions
  • Immunizations given today
  • Vitals from today's visit
  • MyChart sign-up information (if patient not already enrolled)

Generating and Printing the AVS

  1. At the end of the visit, from the navigator or the Check-Out workflow, click After Visit Summary.
  2. Preview the AVS to confirm it is complete and accurate. Check that instructions are patient-friendly and understandable.
  3. Add or modify patient instructions directly in the AVS if needed. Some Ochin templates have a free-text "Provider Notes to Patient" field.
  4. Select the patient's preferred language. Ochin Epic can generate the AVS in multiple languages — Spanish, Vietnamese, Somali, Russian, and others depending on your clinic's configuration. Always use the patient's preferred language.
  5. Print or send electronically. Print for walk-out, or send to patient's MyChart account. Document that AVS was provided in the flowsheet (check "AVS Given" in the visit wrap-up section).
🔑 Regulatory Requirement: Providing the AVS at the time of the visit is a Promoting Interoperability (PI) measure. Clinics that fail to meet the PI threshold for AVS provision can lose Medicaid and Medicare incentive payments. Document "AVS Given: Yes" in the flowsheet for every visit — even if the patient declines to take it.

Check-Out

When the visit is completely finished, the front desk marks the encounter as Check-Out:

  • Right-click the appointment on the schedule → Check Out
  • This updates the appointment status and triggers any remaining billing workflow steps
  • The encounter moves to the billing queue for charge capture and claim submission
  • Epic may prompt for any outstanding items: missing diagnoses, unsigned orders, or missing AVS documentation
💡 Tip — MyChart Enrollment at Check-Out: Check-out is a great opportunity to enroll patients in MyChart if they aren't already. From the patient's chart: Patient → MyChart → Send Activation Code. The patient receives a text or email to set up their account. MyChart-enrolled patients can view their AVS, lab results, and message their care team — improving engagement and reducing phone call volume for your clinic.

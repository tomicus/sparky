🔬 5.1 Computerized Provider Order Entry (CPOE) in Ochin Epic

CPOE is the process of entering all clinical orders — labs, imaging, referrals, procedures, and more — directly into Epic rather than on paper or verbally. At all Ochin member sites, CPOE is mandatory for all orders. This ensures safety, auditability, and proper billing.

Why CPOE matters at Ochin FQHCs: Every order entered in Epic creates a traceable record, triggers billing, alerts the appropriate care team, and routes the request to the right department — all automatically. Paper orders bypass these safety nets and create compliance and billing gaps.

Order Priorities — Know Before You Order

🔴 STAT

Results needed immediately. Used for acute life-threatening situations. Lab processes within 1 hour. Reserve for genuine emergencies — overuse dilutes urgency.

🟡 URGENT

Results needed same day, typically within 2–4 hours. Use for acute but non-critical situations (severe pain, suspected infection with fever).

🟢 ROUTINE

Standard processing time, usually 24–72 hours. The correct choice for most outpatient labs, chronic disease monitoring, and preventive screenings.

🔵 FUTURE

Ordered now but not to be collected immediately — used for "come back fasting" labs, follow-up orders, or pre-visit workup for the next appointment.

Key CPOE Rules in Ochin's Shared Environment

  • All orders must be signed before the patient leaves — unsigned orders do not route to the lab or radiology department and are not billed
  • Verbal orders are only permitted in emergencies — the receiving nurse must immediately enter them into Epic with the "Verbal Order" designation and the ordering provider must co-sign within your clinic's timeframe (usually 24 hours)
  • Do not use "telephone orders" as a workaround — if a provider is on the phone asking for a lab order, they must log in and enter it themselves unless it qualifies as a true verbal order situation
  • Order sets are Ochin's preferred method for complex or multi-order scenarios — they reduce errors and ensure completeness (covered in Section 5.4)
  • Never create duplicate orders — check Pending Orders before entering a new one to avoid sending duplicate lab draws to the patient
⚠️ Shared Instance Warning: In Ochin's shared Epic, orders placed at your clinic are visible to all Ochin-member sites if the patient visits another clinic. This is intentional — it prevents redundant testing. But it also means: if an order from another site is already pending, check before duplicating it.

🧪 5.2 Lab Orders in Ochin Epic

Laboratory orders are the most frequently placed orders at FQHCs. Ochin's Epic includes pre-configured lab compendiums connected to your clinic's contracted lab partner (most commonly LabCorp, Quest, or an in-house lab). Knowing how to search, select, and properly configure lab orders is a core clinical skill.

Placing a Lab Order — Step by Step

  1. Open the patient's encounter
    The encounter must be open to place orders. Navigate to the Order Entry section from the left navigator or press Ctrl+Shift+O.
  2. Click "New Order" or use the search bar
    Type the name of the lab test (e.g., "HbA1c", "CBC", "CMP"). Results appear from Ochin's curated lab compendium — always select from this list, never free-text.
  3. Select the correct test
    Some tests have multiple versions — confirm you're selecting the right panel vs. individual component. Example: "CBC with differential" vs. "CBC without differential" — they are different tests with different costs.
  4. Set the Priority
    Routine, Urgent, or STAT. Default to Routine unless clinical urgency warrants otherwise. STAT must be clinically justified.
  5. Enter the diagnosis (ICD-10)
    Every lab order requires at least one ICD-10 diagnosis code. This is required for billing and medical necessity. Epic will prompt you — do not skip this field.
  6. Set the Collection Date/Time
    For same-day collection, leave as "Now" or "Today." For future orders (e.g., "fasting labs, come back next week"), set the future date and add an instruction.
  7. Add any Collection Instructions
    Use the "Comments" or "Special Instructions" field for patient-specific notes: "Fasting 12 hours," "First morning void for UA," "Trough level — collect 30 min before next dose."
  8. Sign the order
    Click Sign. Unsigned orders do not route to the lab. The order will appear in the Pending Orders list until signed.
💡 Tip: In Ochin Epic, when you sign orders, the system automatically generates a Lab Requisition that can be printed and given to the patient if they are going to an external collection site (e.g., LabCorp patient service center). For in-clinic draws, the nurse receives the order electronically.

Lab Order Errors to Avoid

  • Missing ICD-10 code — the order cannot be billed and may be rejected by the lab. Always enter the diagnosis.
  • Wrong priority — marking everything STAT desensitizes the lab to true urgencies and may cause unnecessary patient anxiety
  • Ordering the wrong panel — "CMP" vs. "BMP" vs. "LFTs" each have different components; confirm what you actually need
  • Forgetting fasting instructions — lipids and glucose need fasting instructions in the order comments, or the patient won't prepare correctly
  • Not printing the requisition — for external lab visits, the patient needs a printed requisition or the lab may not be able to draw the specimen

Point-of-Care (POC) Testing Documentation in Ochin

Many Ochin FQHCs perform POC testing in-clinic: urine dipstick, rapid strep, rapid flu, glucose, pregnancy tests, and others. These are not ordered through the standard lab order screen — they are documented through Flowsheets in Epic.

  1. Navigate to Flowsheets in the patient encounter navigator
  2. Select the appropriate POC flowsheet (e.g., "POC Testing," "Urine Dipstick," "Rapid Strep")
  3. Enter the result directly — the value is stored in the chart and appears in the results section
  4. For waived tests: document the lot number and expiration of the test kit per CLIA waiver requirements — this is required at all FQHC sites
✓ Ochin CLIA Compliance: Point-of-care testing at Ochin-member FQHCs must follow CLIA waiver documentation requirements. Lot numbers, expiration dates, and control results must be documented. Your clinic's CLIA officer will train you on site-specific requirements.

🩻 5.3 Imaging Orders in Ochin Epic

Radiology and imaging orders follow a similar workflow to lab orders but have additional considerations: selecting the correct imaging protocol, coordinating with external radiology partners, and managing prior authorization requirements for certain studies.

Placing an Imaging Order — Step by Step

  1. Open Order Entry and search for the imaging study
    Use clinical terminology: "Chest X-ray," "CT Abdomen Pelvis," "MRI Knee," "Mammogram." Epic will return Ochin's configured imaging compendium.
  2. Select the correct study and laterality (if applicable)
    For extremity imaging, always specify: "X-ray Right Knee" vs. "X-ray Left Knee." For bilateral studies, order separately or select "Bilateral" if available.
  3. Enter the Clinical Indication
    This is separate from the ICD-10 code — it's a brief free-text note explaining WHY you're ordering the study. Radiologists use this to guide their reads. Example: "Persistent right knee pain after fall, rule out fracture."
  4. Select the Radiology Site / Partner
    Ochin clinics have contracted radiology partners. Select the appropriate facility or use your clinic's default. For in-house imaging (X-ray at clinic), this routes to your on-site machine queue.
  5. Set the Priority and Scheduling Instructions
    Routine, Urgent, or STAT. For routine studies, the scheduling team will contact the patient. For STAT, the patient may need to go to radiology directly.
  6. Note Prior Authorization (PA) Requirements
    CT, MRI, PET, and nuclear medicine studies almost always require prior authorization from the patient's insurance. Epic will flag this — do not proceed without addressing it.
  7. Sign the order
    Click Sign. For external imaging, the order generates a requisition. For in-house imaging, it routes to the radiology queue.

Radiology Reports — How They Return

When the radiologist completes their read, the report returns to Epic in one of two ways:

  • Electronic (Preferred): The radiology partner sends the report directly into Epic via HL7/FHIR interface. The report appears in the patient chart under "Results → Imaging" and routes to your In Basket for review.
  • Fax-to-Chart: If the radiology partner is not directly integrated with Ochin's Epic, their staff fax the report to your clinic. The fax is scanned into Epic and attached to the order. Your Ochin analyst will tell you which radiology partners are directly integrated at your site.
💡 Critical Radiology Findings: When a radiologist identifies a critical or urgent finding (e.g., suspected pulmonary embolism, unexpected mass, acute fracture), they are required to contact you directly by phone — this is called a "Critical Call." Document the conversation in the chart: time, who called, what was communicated, and your plan. Then review the full report when it arrives.

📋 5.4 Order Sets & Protocols in Ochin Epic

Order sets are pre-built groups of related orders that can be placed together with a single action. In Ochin's shared Epic environment, order sets are maintained at the Ochin network level and can also be customized per clinic. They represent a critical efficiency and safety tool — especially for complex chronic disease management, preventive care, and pre-procedure workups.

✓ Ochin's Order Set Library: Ochin maintains a curated library of evidence-based order sets aligned with FQHC care delivery, UDS requirements, and HRSA quality measures. Before building a custom order set, check whether Ochin already has one that fits your need — it's likely in the library.

How to Access and Use an Order Set

  1. Open Order Entry in the patient's encounter
  2. Click "Order Sets" (button next to the search bar, or search Order Sets)
  3. Search for the relevant order set by name or clinical condition. Example: "Diabetes Annual Visit," "Well Child 2-Year," "Annual Wellness Visit (AWV)"
  4. Review the order set contents — a checkbox list appears showing all included orders. Uncheck anything that doesn't apply to this specific patient.
  5. Complete any required fields — some orders within the set will require you to enter a diagnosis or priority before signing
  6. Click "Sign All" to place all selected orders simultaneously

Standing Orders — The MA's Role

Standing orders are pre-authorized protocols that allow MAs and nurses to initiate specific orders without waiting for a provider to write them individually. In Ochin's Epic, standing orders are configured at the clinic level and must be approved by the medical director.

Common Ochin Standing Orders Examples:
• Influenza vaccine — MA can administer to eligible patients without individual provider order
• Urine dipstick — MA can perform POC urinalysis on patients presenting with urinary symptoms
• Blood glucose POC — MA can check fingerstick glucose on diabetic patients per rooming protocol
• BMI/weight reassessment — MA documents and initiates BMI-triggered order per standing protocol

Standing orders do NOT mean unsupervised practice — the provider reviews all standing order results and co-signs the encounter. Know your clinic's specific approved standing orders.

📊 5.5 Reviewing Lab & Imaging Results in Ochin Epic

Reviewing results is not just checking a number — it's a clinical act that requires interpretation, patient notification, documentation of your assessment, and follow-up planning. In Ochin's Epic, this process is tracked, auditable, and has direct implications for patient safety and regulatory compliance.

Result Status Flags — Know What Each Means

NORMAL

Within reference range. Still requires provider review and acknowledgment — "normal" doesn't mean "ignore."

HIGH

Above the reference range. Requires clinical interpretation — mildly elevated vs. significantly elevated require different responses.

LOW

Below the reference range. Some LOW values are as dangerous as HIGH (e.g., low sodium, low potassium, low glucose).

CRITICAL

Requires immediate action. The lab calls you directly (Critical Call). Document the call. Contact the patient urgently. Do not just acknowledge and move on.

The Right Way to Review a Result

  1. Open the result from In Basket or the Results navigator
    Read the full result — not just the value, but the reference range, the collection time, and any lab comments or flags.
  2. Interpret clinically in context
    A creatinine of 1.3 means something very different in a 25-year-old vs. a 78-year-old. Reference ranges are population averages — your clinical judgment must contextualize every abnormal value.
  3. Document your assessment
    For significant results, add a note or result comment in Epic: "HbA1c 9.2% — suboptimal control. Increasing metformin dose, patient counseled on diet, follow-up in 3 months."
  4. Communicate with the patient
    Every result requires some form of patient communication. Determine the appropriate method: MyChart message, phone call, letter, or scheduled follow-up visit.
  5. Mark as Reviewed
    Click "Mark as Reviewed" in the In Basket. This removes the result from your pending queue and documents that you have personally reviewed it.
  6. Place follow-up orders if needed
    Abnormal results often trigger additional workup. Document the plan and place any new orders within the same review workflow.
🚨
Critical Values — Non-Negotiable Response Requirements
When a lab calls with a critical value, you must:
1. Receive and document the "read-back" — repeat the value back to the lab tech verbatim
2. Document in Epic: the time of call, the name of the lab tech who called, the value reported, and your clinical response
3. Contact the patient immediately — critical values require same-day patient notification
4. Acknowledge the result in the In Basket

Failure to act on a critical result is a major patient safety event and a CMS/accreditation violation.

📬 5.6 Managing Results in the In Basket

The In Basket is Epic's central hub for all provider tasks and communications — and the Results folder is where new lab and imaging results land for your review. Letting your In Basket fill up with unreviewed results is a patient safety problem and a liability risk. Build a daily habit of clearing your Results In Basket.

⚠️ Results Do Not Read Themselves: In Ochin Epic, a result that has returned but has NOT been reviewed and acknowledged by the ordering provider is considered "unreviewed." Unreviewed results create both patient safety and legal liability for the provider. Ochin support staff and Ochin quality monitors track unreviewed result rates across the network.

The Result Review Workflow in In Basket

  1. Open In Basket — Ctrl + G from anywhere in Epic, or click the In Basket icon in the toolbar
  2. Click the "Results" folder
    Results are organized by patient. A bold number shows how many unreviewed results are waiting. Results are color-coded: critical results appear in red at the top.
  3. Click a result to open it
    The full lab or radiology report opens. Review the values, flags, reference ranges, and any lab comments.
  4. Choose your action from the toolbar:
    Acknowledge — Result reviewed, no further action needed
    Route to Patient — Send to patient via MyChart with optional message
    Add Comment — Add your clinical interpretation to the result
    Route to Staff — Send to a nurse or MA for follow-up action
    Pend — Keep in In Basket if you need to act later (use sparingly)
  5. For abnormal results: Document your plan
    Before acknowledging, click "Add Comment" and document what you are doing: "Result reviewed. HbA1c 9.4% — increasing metformin, patient called and informed, follow-up in 3 months."
  6. For results requiring immediate patient contact
    Call the patient first. Document the call in a Telephone Encounter note. Then acknowledge the result in the In Basket.

Releasing Results to Patients via MyChart

Ochin's Epic is configured with MyChart result release rules. Providers can release results to patients through MyChart directly from the In Basket:

  • Auto-release rules — Your clinic's MyChart configuration determines which result types auto-release and after what delay (commonly 3–7 days for labs, immediately for some providers)
  • Manual release — Click "Route to Patient" in the In Basket and select "Send to MyChart." You can add a patient-friendly message.
  • Sensitive results — HIV, cancer pathology, and certain mental health results may have different release rules. Check your clinic's MyChart configuration and follow your clinic's policy.
  • Patient message tips — When releasing an abnormal result, always include a brief explanation: "Your HbA1c is 9.4%, which is above the goal of 7%. I'll be calling you to discuss next steps."

In Basket Best Practices at Ochin Sites

💡 Daily Habits That Prevent Problems:
• Clear results every day — unreviewed results accumulate quickly. Block 15–20 minutes at the start and end of each clinical day to work through your In Basket.
• Set up coverage — when you are out of clinic (vacation, leave), set a covering provider in your In Basket settings so your results are routed appropriately. Unreviewed results with no coverage create dangerous gaps.
• Use the "Pend" function sparingly — pending a result in your In Basket without action is not a solution. If you can't act immediately, route it to your nurse or MA to facilitate the patient contact, then acknowledge.
• Document every phone notification — if you call a patient about an abnormal result, open a Telephone Encounter note and document the call. This creates the clinical record that the patient was notified.

🔗 5.7 Referrals & Care Everywhere in Ochin Epic

As an FQHC, your clinic is the hub of primary care for your patient population — but patients frequently need specialist care, hospital services, and external diagnostics. Managing referrals and accessing outside records through Care Everywhere are critical care coordination skills in Ochin's Epic.

Placing a Referral Order

  1. Open Order Entry in the patient's encounter
  2. Search for the specialty referral
    Type the specialty: "Cardiology Referral," "Orthopedics Referral," "Behavioral Health Referral." Ochin's order compendium includes referral types configured for your clinic's specialist network.
  3. Select the referring specialty and provider (if known)
    If your clinic has a preferred specialist or a formal relationship with a specialty group, select them from the list. If open to any provider, leave as the specialty pool.
  4. Enter the Clinical Indication and Urgency
    Write a brief, clear reason for the referral. The specialist's office uses this to triage: "T2DM with HbA1c 10.2% despite max oral therapy — refer to endocrinology for insulin initiation and management."
  5. Attach relevant clinical information
    In the referral order, you can attach the last visit note, relevant labs, or a specific document. This is critical — a referral sent without supporting information delays the specialist appointment.
  6. Set the Urgency
    Routine (standard scheduling), Urgent (within 1–2 weeks), or STAT (same day — for referrals that are essentially emergency transfers).
  7. Indicate Prior Authorization need
    Most specialist referrals require insurance PA. Flag this in the order so your referral coordinator knows to initiate the PA process before scheduling.
  8. Sign the referral order
    The referral routes to your clinic's referral coordinator, who will contact the specialist office, obtain PA, and schedule the patient. Alternatively, your clinic may have a Direct Scheduling arrangement with certain specialists.

Tracking Referral Status in Ochin Epic

One of the most critical care coordination functions is ensuring patients actually complete their referrals — this is called "closing the loop." Epic tracks referral status, and Ochin has specific workflows for this.

Unresolved referrals with no follow-up represent a patient safety gap and are tracked as a quality metric at Ochin FQHCs.

Care Everywhere — Accessing External Records

Care Everywhere is Epic's interoperability network that allows clinicians to view records from other Epic organizations and health information exchanges (HIEs). In Ochin's shared instance, this gives you access to a patient's records from hospitals, specialist offices, urgent care centers, and other Epic-based health systems nationwide.

How to Use Care Everywhere

  1. Open the patient chart
  2. Look for the "Care Everywhere" indicator
    A banner or button in the chart header shows if external records are available. You may also see a globe icon 🌐 in the chart navigator.
  3. Click "External Records" or "Care Everywhere"
    Epic queries the network for records linked to this patient. This may take a few seconds. Results show the participating organizations that have records for this patient.
  4. Select the organization and review available records
    You can see external medications, problem lists, allergies, visit notes, lab results, and imaging reports. Not all record types are available from every organization.
  5. Selectively pull records into your chart
    You can reconcile external medications, pull in problem list items, or view notes. "Pulling in" creates a copy in your chart that you can act on. Always review before pulling — not all external data is current or accurate.
  6. Document your review
    Note in your visit documentation that you reviewed Care Everywhere records: "External records reviewed via Care Everywhere — no significant changes to medications or problem list identified."

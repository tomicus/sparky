🔍 2.1 Searching for Existing Patients

Before registering anyone as a new patient, always search first. Duplicate medical records are a significant problem in Epic — they require formal merge requests and create patient safety risks. A thorough search prevents duplicates.

How to Open Patient Search

  • Press Ctrl + E from anywhere in Epic, OR
  • Click Patient Search in the top toolbar, OR
  • Click the ⚡ Epic Button → Patient Search

Step-by-Step: Searching for a Patient

  1. Open Patient Search (Ctrl + E) The search dialog opens. You'll see fields for Last Name, First Name, DOB, MRN, and more.
  2. Enter Last Name + Date of Birth This combination catches most existing patients even with name spelling variations. Example: Last Name Garcia, DOB 03/22/1978
  3. Click Search or press Enter A results list appears. Review all results — do not just click the first match.
  4. Verify identity before opening the chart Confirm: full name, DOB, address, and last 4 of SSN if available. Never rely on name alone — two patients may have the same name and similar DOB.
  5. If multiple results appear Check each record. Look for the one with the most complete information, matching insurance, and your clinic's organization listed. If you see two records that appear to be the same person, do NOT merge — report to your supervisor or the Ochin Help Desk.
  6. If no match is found Try alternate spellings, maiden names, nicknames, and different DOB formats before proceeding to new registration. Ask the patient to spell their name and confirm their DOB verbally.

💡 Tip — Soundex Search: Ochin Epic's search uses phonetic matching. If a patient's name is spelled differently than expected (e.g., "Gonzales" vs "Gonzalez"), try searching with just the first few letters and DOB to catch spelling variations.
⚠️ MPI Overlap Alert: Because Ochin is a shared environment, a patient may have visited a different Ochin member clinic before yours. You will see their record with that other clinic's organization. You can still open their chart and add your organization — but do NOT create a new patient record for them.

Guarantor and Emergency Contact

  1. Guarantor The person financially responsible for the account. For adults, the guarantor is usually Self. For minors, the guarantor is typically a parent or guardian. In Epic: Registration → Guarantor tab → set relationship and enter guarantor's information if different from patient.
  2. Emergency Contact Name, relationship, and phone number. Required for all patients. Ask: "In case of emergency, who should we contact?"
  3. Authorized Representatives If a parent, guardian, or healthcare proxy should have access to this patient's information, document them here. This affects MyChart access and who can call for information.

Saving the Registration

Click Accept (or the checkmark icon) to save. Epic will assign a new MRN automatically. Write down or print the MRN for the patient's wristband or check-in paperwork if your clinic uses printed labels.

💡 Tip — Preferred Name: Always ask "What name do you go by?" and enter it in the Preferred Name field. Providers see this name in the chart header, which reduces misgendering and improves patient experience. The legal name is still used for billing and insurance.

💳 2.3 Insurance Verification in Ochin Epic

Accurate insurance coverage information is essential for billing. In Ochin Epic, insurance is managed in the Coverage section of the patient's registration. You must verify insurance at every visit — coverage changes frequently.

Where to Find Coverage

From the patient's chart: Registration → Coverage tab. Or from check-in: the system prompts you to verify coverage during the check-in workflow automatically.

Adding a New Insurance Plan

  1. Click "Add Coverage" in the Coverage tab A new coverage entry form opens.
  2. Search for the Payor (Insurance Company) Type the insurance company name in the Payor field. Ochin Epic has a pre-built payor library. Select the exact plan — e.g., "Medicaid Oregon (OHP)" not just "Medicaid." If you cannot find the payor, contact your billing department before creating a new one.
  3. Enter Member ID and Group Number Copy exactly from the insurance card. Member ID is the patient's individual ID. Group number identifies the employer or plan group (often blank for Medicaid/Medicare).
  4. Enter Effective and Termination Dates From the insurance card or eligibility check. Leave Termination Date blank if coverage is ongoing.
  5. Set the Coverage Sequence Primary = 1, Secondary = 2, Tertiary = 3. This tells Epic which insurance to bill first. Incorrect sequencing causes claim denials.
  6. Enter Subscriber Information If the patient is the policyholder, select "Self." If a spouse or parent holds the plan, enter the subscriber's name, DOB, and relationship. This is required for billing.
  7. Run Electronic Eligibility Verification Click Verify Eligibility (or the checkmark icon in the coverage row). Epic sends a real-time 270/271 transaction to the payor and returns coverage details within seconds.

Common Insurance Scenarios at Ochin FQHCs

Medicaid / OHP (Oregon Health Plan)

  • Most common coverage at Ochin member clinics
  • Verify the specific Coordinated Care Organization (CCO) — e.g., CareOregon, PacificSource, Health Share. The CCO determines which specialists and labs are covered
  • OHP covers most preventive care at $0 cost to the patient

Medicare

  • Part A = hospital/inpatient. Part B = outpatient office visits (relevant for FQHC billing)
  • FQHCs bill Medicare at the all-inclusive FQHC PPS rate — different from standard Medicare billing
  • Check for a Medicare Advantage plan (Part C) — these may have different networks and cost-sharing

Self-Pay / Uninsured

  • Document as "Self-Pay" in the coverage section
  • Patient will be evaluated for Sliding Fee Scale (see Section 2.4)
  • Offer to help patient apply for Medicaid or marketplace coverage — document assistance provided
💡 Tip — Scan Insurance Cards: Ochin Epic allows you to scan and attach insurance card images directly to the patient's registration. Use your clinic's scanner and attach via the paperclip icon in the Coverage section. This creates a reference if there are billing questions later.

💲 2.4 Sliding Fee Scale (SFS) in Ochin Epic

The Sliding Fee Scale is a core FQHC requirement. Under federal law (Section 330), FQHCs must offer discounted or free services to patients whose income falls below 200% of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL). Ochin Epic has a built-in Sliding Fee Scale module that automates discount calculations once income is documented.

🔑 Federal Requirement: No patient may be denied care due to inability to pay. FQHCs are required by law to apply the sliding fee scale. Failure to apply it correctly can result in federal audit findings and repayment demands.

Understanding the Discount Levels

Ochin Epic uses a tiered discount system based on household income as a percentage of the Federal Poverty Level. Each year, the FPL is updated by HHS — Ochin updates the thresholds in Epic automatically.

LevelIncome (% of FPL)Discount AppliedExample (family of 3, 2026)
Level A — Full Discount 0% – 100% FPL 100% — Patient pays $0 (or nominal fee only) < ~$24,860/year
Level B — High Discount 101% – 150% FPL ~75–80% discount ~$24,861 – $37,290/year
Level C — Moderate Discount 151% – 200% FPL ~50% discount ~$37,291 – $49,720/year
Level D — Minimal Discount >200% FPL (insured) Billed at full rate (insurance primary) > ~$49,720/year

* Exact thresholds vary by year and family size. These are illustrative examples based on 2026 HHS FPL guidelines.

How to Document Sliding Fee Eligibility in Epic

  1. Open the Sliding Fee Application From the patient's registration: Registration → Financial Assistance tab, or from the check-in workflow when a Self-Pay or low-income flag appears. Your clinic may call this the "SFS Application" or "Discount Application."
  2. Document Household Size Ask: "How many people live in your household?" Include the patient, their spouse/partner, dependent children, and any others financially dependent on the household. Enter the total number.
  3. Document Gross Annual Household Income Ask: "What is your total household income before taxes?" Include all income sources: wages, Social Security, disability, child support, etc. If the patient does not know the annual total, ask for monthly and multiply by 12, or weekly and multiply by 52. Enter in the Annual Income field.
  4. Select Income Documentation Type Self-declaration is acceptable at most Ochin clinics — the patient verbally states income and signs the form. If your clinic requires documentation, accepted forms include: recent pay stubs, prior year tax return, employer letter, SSI/SSDI award letter, or zero-income declaration form.
  5. Epic Calculates the Discount Level Automatically Based on household size and income, Epic assigns the appropriate discount level (A, B, C, or D) and displays it on the screen. Confirm with the patient and explain what they will owe.
  6. Set the Expiration Date Sliding fee applications expire — typically every 12 months. Epic will flag expired applications at future check-ins. Enter the expiration date (usually today + 1 year, per your clinic's policy).
  7. Print and Have Patient Sign the SFS Agreement The patient must sign acknowledging their discount level and any nominal fee owed. Scan the signed form and attach it to the patient's registration, or document that it was signed in the SFS application note field.

Sliding Fee: Common Questions & Situations

Patient refuses to disclose income

Document "Patient declined to provide income information." Per FQHC policy, patients who decline are typically assigned the highest discount level (Level A) or billed at full rate depending on your clinic's policy. Consult your billing supervisor. Never deny care.

Patient has insurance AND low income

Insurance is billed first. The sliding fee applies to the patient's out-of-pocket portion after insurance pays — copays, coinsurance, and deductibles may be discounted based on the SFS level. This is an important distinction for Medicaid patients who may have a small copay.

SFS application is expired

Epic will flag this at check-in. Complete a new application before or during the current visit. If the patient cannot complete it today, document the attempt and follow up.

💡 Tip — Privacy: Income and household information is sensitive. Conduct the SFS conversation privately — not at an open front desk where other patients can hear. Many clinics use a private window, phone call, or online pre-registration form to collect this information before the patient arrives.

✅ 2.5 The Check-In Workflow

When a patient arrives for their scheduled appointment, the Check-In process updates their status in Epic from "Scheduled" to "Arrived" and prompts staff to verify all registration information. This is one of the most frequently performed tasks in Ochin Epic.

Accessing the Check-In Workflow

  • From the Schedule: Open the schedule for your clinic/provider, right-click on the patient's appointment → Check In, OR
  • From the Appointment Desk: Epic Button → Appointment Desk → find the patient → Check In button, OR
  • From Patient Search: Open patient → Appointments tab → highlight today's appointment → Check In

Full Check-In Step-by-Step

🔍 Step 1: Open Schedule & Find Patient
Locate the patient's appointment on today's schedule. Right-click → Check In.
👤 Step 2: Verify Patient Identity
Confirm name, DOB, and address verbally. Ask for photo ID if your clinic requires it.
📋 Step 3: Review & Update Demographics
Epic presents a demographics summary. Confirm address, phone, emergency contact. Update any changes. Click Accept.
💳 Step 4: Verify Insurance Coverage
Review coverage list. Run eligibility check. Add or update coverage as needed. Confirm coverage sequence.
💲 Step 5: Review Sliding Fee Status
If SFS on file: confirm not expired. If expired or new patient: complete SFS application. If insured: skip or apply to out-of-pocket.
📝 Step 6: Consent & Questionnaires
Confirm annual consent for treatment on file. Present any required intake questionnaires (PHQ-9, AUDIT, etc.).
✅ Step 7: Arrive the Appointment
Click "Arrive" to change patient status. The provider's schedule will now show the patient as Arrived (green/yellow indicator).
💰 Step 8: Collect Copay / Nominal Fee
Collect any copay, coinsurance, or SFS nominal fee. Post payment in Epic → Patient → Payments. Print receipt.

Posting a Copay Payment

  1. From the check-in workflow or patient chart: Go to Patient → Payments or click the Collect Payment button that appears at the end of check-in.
  2. Enter the payment amount and method: Cash, Check, Credit/Debit Card, or Money Order. Select from the dropdown. Enter amount.
  3. Apply to the correct account/visit: Epic will suggest the current visit. Confirm it's applying to the right encounter.
  4. Print receipt: Always offer a receipt. Epic generates one automatically — click Print Receipt.
  5. Zero-dollar copay visits: If the patient owes $0 (Level A SFS or Medicaid with no copay), still click through the payment screen and document $0.00 collected. This closes the payment step and allows the visit to proceed.
💡 Tip — Kiosk Check-In: Some Ochin clinics have patient self-service kiosks (Epic MyChart Bedside or third-party kiosks). When a patient checks in on the kiosk, their status changes to "Arrived" automatically. Staff should still review the demographics and coverage queue in Epic to confirm accuracy.
⚠️ Never skip the Arrive step. If a patient is not "Arrived" in Epic, providers may not know they are in the building. The appointment will appear on no-show reports. Always complete the full check-in workflow — even if the patient is already in the exam room.

📋 2.6 Consent Forms & Patient Documents

Ochin Epic manages patient consent and forms through the eConsent module and the patient's chart Documents section. Most clinics require a fresh consent for treatment annually, and specific consents for procedures, telehealth, or release of information.

How to Document Consent in Ochin Epic

  1. Navigate to eConsent From the patient's chart: Chart → Consents tab, or during check-in the system may prompt for outstanding consents automatically.
  2. Review Outstanding Consents Epic shows a list of required consents with status: Obtained, Expired, or Missing. Address any Missing or Expired items.
  3. Obtain Signature Option A: Digital signature on a tablet or signature pad connected to the workstation.
    Option B: Print the form, obtain wet signature, scan it back, and attach it to the patient's chart using the paperclip / Media Manager.
  4. Document in Epic In the Consents tab, change the status to Obtained and enter the date. If paper-signed, add a note: "Signed paper copy scanned to chart."

Attaching Scanned Documents

Scanned documents (insurance cards, signed consents, ID copies) are stored in Epic's Media Manager:

  • Patient chart → Media tab (or search "Media Manager" in Epic Button)
  • Click Acquire → scan directly or upload a file from your workstation
  • Select the document Type from the dropdown (Insurance Card, Consent Form, Photo ID, etc.)
  • Add a Description and click Accept
  • Documents appear in the Media tab and can be viewed by any authorized staff member
Release of Information (ROI): When a patient or external provider requests medical records, use Epic's ROI module — not email or fax without proper authorization. The ROI module tracks the request, documents authorization, applies HIPAA minimum-necessary rules, and generates an audit trail. Never release records without a completed ROI authorization form.

Minors and Guardian Consent

  • For patients under 18, consent is typically provided by a parent or legal guardian
  • In some states, minors may consent for specific services independently (reproductive health, mental health, substance use) — check your state's minor consent laws and your clinic's policy
  • In Epic, document the consenting guardian in the Guarantor / Authorized Representatives section of registration
  • Emancipated minors can consent for themselves — document emancipation status in Registration → Legal
💡 Tip — Consent Expiration Alerts: During check-in, if a patient's General Consent for Treatment has expired, a yellow alert will appear in the check-in workflow. Do not skip it — take two minutes to have the patient sign a new consent before they are roomed.

🚶 2.7 Walk-In Patients & No-Shows

Handling Walk-In Patients

Walk-in patients (no scheduled appointment) are common at community health centers. Ochin Epic handles them through Add-On appointments or Walk-In slots on the schedule.

  1. Search for the patient first Always search for an existing record before creating a new one (see Section 2.1).
  2. Determine if a walk-in slot is available Open the schedule for the desired provider or department. Look for designated "Walk-In" or "Open Access" slots. If none are available, check with the charge nurse or supervisor.
  3. Schedule an Add-On appointment Right-click on an empty slot on the schedule → Schedule Appointment. Select visit type Walk-In or Add-On (your clinic may have specific visit types for this). Add a brief chief complaint in the appointment notes.
  4. Complete the full check-in workflow Follow all steps in Section 2.5: demographics, insurance, sliding fee, consent, arrive the appointment.
  5. Notify the provider or charge nurse Communicate that a walk-in has been added to the schedule. Use the Epic In Basket → Staff Message or call the nurse directly.
💡 Open Access Scheduling: Many Ochin FQHCs use an Open Access scheduling model where a percentage of each day's slots are held for same-day appointments. These slots open at a set time each morning. Know your clinic's open access policy — it affects how you manage walk-ins.

Marking a No-Show

When a patient does not arrive for their scheduled appointment and does not call to cancel, mark them as No Show in Epic. This is typically done after a set waiting period (commonly 15–30 minutes past the appointment time, per clinic policy).

  1. From the Schedule: Right-click the patient's appointment → No Show
  2. Select a reason from the dropdown: Patient No-Call/No-Show, Patient Called to Cancel (late), Transportation Issue, etc. Document the reason — it populates no-show reports.
  3. Epic changes the appointment status to "No Show" (red indicator). The slot may now be available for another patient depending on your clinic's scheduling rules.
  4. Attempt to contact the patient per your clinic's no-show protocol. Some clinics have an MA or front desk staff call the patient. Document the outreach attempt in a telephone encounter note if required.
  5. Reschedule if appropriate Right-click the no-show appointment → Reschedule, or ask the patient to call back to reschedule when you reach them.
⚠️ Never cancel instead of no-showing. Marking an appointment as Cancelled when the patient simply did not show removes the appointment from no-show reports and distorts your clinic's data. Use No Show for patients who did not arrive. Use Cancel only when the patient or clinic initiated the cancellation in advance.

Late Cancellations

SituationAction in Epic
Patient calls to cancel >24 hours in advanceCancel appointment → reason "Patient Cancelled." Offer to reschedule.
Patient calls to cancel same-day (<24 hours)Cancel with reason "Same-Day Cancellation." Document time of call in appointment notes.

HIPAA and Appointment Reminders

Ochin Epic can automatically send appointment reminders via phone call, text message, or MyChart notification. Before activating these, ensure:

  • The patient has consented to text reminders (documented in registration communication preferences)
  • Reminder messages do not include sensitive information (e.g., "Your behavioral health appointment is tomorrow" — this reveals the appointment type)
  • Your clinic has configured reminder messaging per HIPAA minimum-necessary standards

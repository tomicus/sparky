Scheduling

Questions 51–60
51
A provider is out sick today. How do I block their schedule and handle existing appointments?
Scheduling
▼
Answer
Navigate to the provider's schedule → click on the time blocks you want to close → select Add Block and choose "Provider Out" or equivalent reason → this prevents new same-day scheduling. Then review all existing appointments for that day: contact patients to reschedule or offer care with an available provider. Epic's appointment desk lets you view all today's appointments for a provider in a list. For urgent patients who can't wait, check whether another provider has capacity or refer to urgent care appropriately.
Ochin Note: For mass rescheduling of many patients at once (e.g., provider out for a week), contact the Ochin Help Desk — they have tools to perform bulk appointment actions that individual clinic staff cannot do. Don't manually cancel 40 appointments one by one if a bulk tool is available.
52
How do I add a patient to the waitlist when no appointments are available?
Scheduling
▼
Answer
From the scheduling dialog, when no slots are available, click Add to Waitlist. Document: preferred provider, appointment type needed, urgency level, the patient's available date range, and best contact number. When a slot opens due to a cancellation, schedulers review the waitlist by urgency and date preference and contact patients in order. Use Schedule from Waitlist to book the patient directly without re-entering information. Review and clean the waitlist daily — remove patients who have been scheduled or who found care elsewhere.
💡 Tip: Set a calendar reminder to review the waitlist every morning. Yesterday's cancellations are today's open slots — a proactive scheduler fills gaps before they become no-shows.
53
What is the correct disposition when a patient cancels with less than 24 hours' notice vs. a true no-show?
Scheduling
▼
Answer
Both are still Cancelled — Patient in Epic, regardless of how close to the appointment they called. A cancellation means the patient called at all — even one hour before. A No-Show is reserved only for patients who gave no notice whatsoever and simply didn't appear. Your clinic may track late cancellations separately in appointment notes for internal policy purposes (e.g., "Cancelled same-day — called 30 min before"), but the Epic disposition remains Cancelled, not No-Show. This distinction matters for UDS access metrics.
54
A patient needs a recurring appointment every 4 weeks. Do I have to schedule each one individually?
Scheduling
▼
Answer
No — use Epic's Recurring/Series scheduling feature. When scheduling, look for the "Recurrence" or "Series" option in the scheduling dialog. Set the frequency (every 4 weeks), number of occurrences, and end date. Epic will create all appointments simultaneously, filling available slots that match the appointment type. Note: not all appointment types support series scheduling in Ochin's build — if the option isn't visible, check with your super user or schedule them in batches (e.g., 3 at a time).
Real-World Example
A patient starting suboxone needs monthly check-ins for 6 months. The scheduler uses series scheduling to create 6 appointments every 28 days, filling slots on the provider's template. All 6 appointments appear in the patient's MyChart immediately.
55
How do I schedule a patient to see both a primary care provider and a behavioral health clinician on the same day?
Scheduling
▼
Answer
Create two separate appointments — one with the PCP and one with the BH clinician — on the same day. Each appointment uses its own appointment type (Primary Care Follow-Up and BH Follow-Up, for example). Each generates a separate encounter in Epic, with separate notes and separate billing. This is the integrated behavioral health (IBH) model at Ochin FQHCs, and Epic is designed to support it. Coordinate timing so the patient flows from one visit to the next without long waits.
Ochin Billing Note: Two separate qualifying encounters on the same day (PCP + BH) can generate two PPS payments — one for each provider. Both providers must sign their own notes. This is a significant revenue opportunity for integrated clinics — don't undercount by combining into one encounter.
56
How do I view a provider's full schedule for the week at once?
Scheduling
▼
Answer
Navigate to Scheduling → Provider Schedule → select the provider → switch the view toggle from "Day" to "Week." This shows all scheduled appointments across the full week in a grid view. You can click any appointment to see details, reschedule, or check in. For a multi-provider view, use the Department Schedule view, which shows all providers in your department side by side for the day — useful for charge nurses and scheduling coordinators during busy clinic mornings.
57
A patient needs an appointment urgently but the provider's schedule is full. What are my options in Epic?
Scheduling
▼
Answer
Options in order of preference: (1) Check for same-day hold slots — most Ochin clinic templates have reserved same-day urgent slots; (2) Check other providers in your clinic with availability using Find Opening across all providers; (3) Add to Waitlist with "Urgent" priority for same-day; (4) Ask the provider if they can add the patient as an overbook — this requires authorization; (5) Telephone triage — have a nurse or provider do a telephone encounter to assess acuity and determine if an ED referral is more appropriate than waiting.
58
How do I document that a patient needs an interpreter when scheduling?
Scheduling
▼
Answer
When scheduling the appointment, look for the Interpreter Needed field or a language/accommodation field in the scheduling dialog. Select the language needed and check the interpreter checkbox. This creates a visible flag on the appointment in the schedule view so rooming staff see it before the patient arrives. Additionally, confirm the patient's preferred language is set correctly in their demographics — this drives multiple downstream functions including AVS language and MyChart communications.
⚠️ Don't wait until check-in to arrange an interpreter. By then it's too late for in-person interpretation services. Document at scheduling so your interpreter services coordinator can have a qualified interpreter ready.
59
What is the difference between "Find Opening" and "Schedule Appointment" in Epic?
Scheduling
▼
Answer
Find Opening is a search tool — it searches all available slots across a date range that match your criteria (appointment type, provider, department) and returns a list of the next available options. It's best when you need to find when the patient can come in. Schedule Appointment (without Find Opening) goes directly to the provider's schedule so you can manually choose a specific slot — best when you already know the date and time you want. For most scheduling interactions, use Find Opening first to identify availability, then confirm with the patient.
60
How do appointment reminder settings work and who controls them?
Scheduling
▼
Answer
Ochin configures automated appointment reminders at the network level — the timing, channels (call/text/MyChart), and message content are managed by Ochin in partnership with your clinic leadership. Individual staff cannot change reminder timing or content. However, you influence reminder effectiveness by: keeping patient phone numbers and preferred contact method current in demographics, capturing text consent during registration, and ensuring patients have active MyChart accounts. Patient-level communication preferences (call vs. text vs. email) are set in their demographics and drive which channel is used.
Ochin Note: If your clinic wants to change reminder timing or add a new channel (e.g., add SMS reminders), submit a request through my.ochin.org. These are network-level configuration changes that Ochin manages — not something clinic staff can change in Epic directly.

Billing & Coding

Questions 61–70
61
Why does my note need to be signed before billing can process the claim?
Billing
▼
Answer
A signed note is the legal attestation that you — the provider — personally performed and documented the services billed. CMS and all payers require that claims be supported by a signed encounter note in the medical record. Until you sign, the encounter is in "draft" status and cannot be billed. The billing team literally cannot touch an unsigned encounter. Every day a note sits unsigned is a day closer to missing the payer's timely filing deadline — potentially losing that revenue permanently.
Real-World Example
A provider sees 18 patients on Monday but signs notes for only 14 before leaving. The billing team can only process 14 claims. The 4 unsigned notes sit in limbo. If the provider doesn't sign them until Thursday, and one patient had a 3-day timely filing window with their insurer (rare but possible), that claim may be denied. Sign your notes the same day.
62
What is the difference between a 99213 and a 99214 visit, and how does my documentation determine which one applies?
Billing
▼
Answer
Both are established patient E&M codes. 99213 = Low complexity (1–2 stable chronic conditions or minor acute problem; limited data review; low-risk management like OTC medications). 99214 = Moderate complexity (chronic condition with exacerbation, new problem with uncertain prognosis, or 2+ chronic conditions requiring prescription management; moderate data review; prescription drug management). The key driver since 2021 is Medical Decision Making (MDM) complexity or total time. If you managed a patient's uncontrolled diabetes AND their new knee pain AND reviewed external cardiology records in one visit — that's 99214 or higher. Document what you actually did.
💡 Time-Based Alternative: If your total time spent on the encounter (including documentation) on the date of service was 30–39 minutes, that supports 99214 regardless of MDM. Document: "Total time including documentation: 35 minutes." Epic has a time entry field in the encounter — use it.
63
What is a "qualifying FQHC visit" and what makes a visit NOT qualify?
Billing
▼
Answer
A qualifying visit requires: (1) face-to-face contact (or qualifying telehealth) with a billable FQHC provider (MD, DO, NP, PA, CNM, or licensed BH clinician); (2) at the HRSA-designated FQHC service site; (3) a signed encounter note; (4) at least one ICD-10 diagnosis. Visits that do NOT qualify: care provided by non-billable staff only (MA visit without provider contact), phone calls that don't meet qualifying telehealth criteria, administrative-only encounters, or services at a non-designated site. Understanding this matters because each qualifying visit generates one PPS payment.
⚠️ Common Mistake: An MA taking vitals and updating the medication list, with the provider never entering the room or signing a note, is NOT a qualifying FQHC visit. A provider must have direct patient contact and sign a note documenting their assessment and plan.
64
A claim was denied for "not medically necessary." How does documentation prevent this?
Billing
▼
Answer
Medical necessity denials happen when the diagnosis code(s) on the claim don't logically justify the service billed. Prevention: (1) Every lab order needs an ICD-10 that explains why the test is needed — "HbA1c" ordered with E11.9 (Type 2 DM) makes clinical sense; the same test ordered with Z00.00 (routine exam) may be denied; (2) E&M level must match documentation — a 99215 requires documented high-complexity MDM; (3) Imaging needs a clinical indication — "MRI Knee" needs a diagnosis that justifies MRI (not just "knee pain" — be specific about findings, duration, and what conservative treatment has failed).
Real-World Example
A provider orders a lipid panel. The ICD-10 on the order is Z00.00 (routine exam). The lab bills the insurer with Z00.00. Insurer denies: "lipid panel not covered under routine exam." If the provider had used E78.5 (hyperlipidemia) or I10 (HTN — risk factor screening), the claim would have been paid. The diagnosis code is the clinical justification for the test.
65
Can we bill for a wellness visit AND a problem-focused visit on the same day?
Billing
▼
Answer
Yes — with conditions. If a patient comes for an Annual Wellness Visit (AWV) or Annual Physical AND you address a separate, significant acute or chronic problem during the same visit, you can bill both the preventive code AND an E&M code, using Modifier 25 on the E&M to indicate it was a significant, separately identifiable service. The key: the problem-focused service must be documented separately from the preventive exam content, and it must be a genuinely separate clinical problem — not just a mention of a chronic condition being managed.
Ochin Note: Medicare AWV (G0438/G0439) is frequently combined with an E&M at FQHCs. Your billing team applies Modifier 25 during charge review. The provider's job is clear documentation: "Today's visit included an Annual Wellness Visit AND a separate evaluation of the patient's uncontrolled hypertension requiring medication adjustment."
66
What is the Sliding Fee Scale and who qualifies for it?
Billing
▼
Answer
The Sliding Fee Scale (SFS) is an HRSA-required income-based discount program. Any patient — regardless of insurance status — who has income at or below 200% of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL) may qualify for reduced fees. Uninsured patients at or below 100% FPL must be offered services at a nominal fee or no charge. The SFS applies to the patient's out-of-pocket costs — copays, deductibles, self-pay balances. It does NOT affect what the clinic bills to insurance. Eligibility is determined at registration using income and household size documentation (pay stubs, tax returns, or self-attestation per your clinic's policy).
67
What are G-codes and when are they used at FQHCs?
Billing
▼
Answer
G-codes are HCPCS Level II codes used primarily for Medicare billing. At FQHCs specifically: G0466–G0470 are the FQHC visit type codes for Medicare (new patient, established, initial preventive, AWV, mental health). These are used instead of standard CPT codes when billing Medicare for FQHC encounters. G0071 is the Medicare FQHC telehealth encounter code. Ochin's Epic is configured to automatically apply the correct G-codes for Medicare FQHC encounters during charge review — you don't select them manually, but understanding what they are helps you recognize when billing is working correctly.
68
A patient has both Medicare and Medicaid (dual eligible). How does billing work?
Billing
▼
Answer
For dual-eligible patients, Medicare is always billed first (primary payer) and Medicaid is billed second (secondary payer). In Epic, both coverages should be active in the patient's account with Medicare listed as primary. Epic's billing engine handles the crossover claim automatically — Medicare pays its portion and Medicaid typically covers the remaining patient responsibility (copay, deductible). Dual-eligible patients generally owe little to nothing out of pocket. Verify both coverages during check-in and confirm primary/secondary ordering is correct.
Ochin Note: Dual-eligible patients are common at FQHCs due to the population served. Make sure both plans are entered in Epic with correct priority (Medicare primary, Medicaid secondary). An incorrectly ordered coverage causes billing errors that take weeks to resolve.
69
How do I know if a claim was paid, denied, or is still pending?
Billing
▼
Answer
Clinical staff generally don't need to track individual claim status — that's the billing team's role. However, if you need to check: in the patient's account → navigate to Account/Billing → Transactions or Claims tab → you can see charges, payments, and any denial codes posted. The billing team monitors claim status in Epic's billing module and will reach out to providers when documentation clarification is needed to resolve a denial. If a patient asks about a bill or a denial, refer them to your billing department — don't interpret EOBs without billing team context.
70
What documentation is required to support a 99215 (highest-level established patient visit)?
Billing
▼
Answer
A 99215 requires High complexity MDM OR 60+ minutes total time. High complexity MDM requires at least two of three: (1) Problems: one or more chronic illnesses with severe exacerbation, or a new problem posing a threat to life/bodily function; (2) Data: extensive data review including independent interpretation of a test, discussion of management with external provider, or independent review of external records; (3) Risk: drug therapy requiring intensive monitoring, or decision regarding hospitalization. If using time: "Total time including documentation on date of service: 65 minutes." Document specifically — vague notes don't support high-level billing even if the encounter was clinically complex.
Documentation That Supports 99215
"Patient with ESRD, uncontrolled T2DM (HbA1c 11.2%), and newly identified cardiac arrhythmia on EKG today. Independently reviewed today's EKG (new A-fib with RVR — initiating anticoagulation). Reviewed external cardiology records from OHSU (pulled via Care Everywhere). Discussed management plan with on-call cardiologist. Initiating apixaban with close monitoring given renal function. High complexity MDM."

Telehealth

Questions 71–80
71
A patient doesn't have a smartphone or computer. Can they still have a telehealth visit?
Telehealth
▼
Answer
Yes — through an audio-only (telephone) visit, if permitted for the visit type by the patient's insurance. Audio-only visits are scheduled using a telephone visit appointment type and billed with CPT codes 99441–99443 (by time) rather than standard E&M codes. Coverage and reimbursement rates for audio-only are lower than video visits and vary significantly by payer. Document clearly that the visit was audio-only and why video wasn't possible ("Patient does not have access to video-capable device"). For patients who want video but lack technology, some clinics offer a tablet lending program or in-clinic video visit option.
Ochin Note: Audio-only telehealth coverage has changed significantly since COVID-era waivers ended. Always verify with your billing team whether audio-only is covered for a specific patient's plan before conducting the visit. Don't assume coverage based on what was permitted during the public health emergency.
72
Why do I have to ask where the patient is located during a telehealth visit?
Telehealth
▼
Answer
This is a state medical licensing requirement — not optional documentation. Providers must be licensed in the state where the patient is physically located at the time of the telehealth visit. If your patient is visiting family in California and you are only licensed in Oregon, you cannot legally conduct that telehealth visit in California. Document the patient's city and state at the start of every telehealth visit: "Patient located in Portland, OR at time of visit." This is your licensure compliance documentation.
⚠️ Real Risk: Providers have faced state medical board investigations for conducting telehealth visits with patients who were out of state at the time — even if the patient didn't mention it. Ask every time. Document every time. "Same state as always" is not documentation.
73
How do I start a MyChart video visit with a patient in Epic?
Telehealth
▼
Answer
At the time of the scheduled video visit: open the patient's encounter in Epic → look for the Video Visit button or banner (usually in the top of the encounter or the visit toolbar) → click Begin Video Visit. This opens the video connection through the browser or Epic's integrated video module. The patient must have joined from their MyChart app first — confirm they're in the virtual waiting room before you begin. If using a third-party platform (Doximity, Zoom for Healthcare), the launch button connects to that platform's interface instead.
💡 Test Your Setup: Before your first telehealth visit, test your camera and microphone in the video platform. Nothing derails a telehealth visit faster than a provider who can't get their audio working. Ochin support can help you test the setup before go-live.
74
What is the telehealth consent requirement and when does it need to be collected?
Telehealth
▼
Answer
Telehealth consent must be obtained before or at the time of the first telehealth visit — and many states require it to be re-obtained if there is a material change in services. Ochin's Epic can send the telehealth consent via MyChart for the patient to sign digitally before the visit. Alternatively, it can be obtained verbally at the start of the visit and documented: "Verbal consent for telehealth services obtained — patient verbalized understanding of the limitations and agreed to proceed." The consent form covers: nature of telehealth, limitations vs. in-person care, privacy protections, and right to discontinue.
75
Can I prescribe controlled substances via telehealth?
Telehealth
▼
Answer
This depends on the substance and current DEA rules. During and after the COVID public health emergency, the DEA temporarily allowed prescribing controlled substances via telehealth without an in-person evaluation. These rules have been changing — as of the current regulatory environment, Schedule III–V substances (including buprenorphine for OUD) can typically be initiated via telehealth under the Ryan Haight Act telemedicine exceptions. Schedule II substances generally still require an in-person evaluation before initial prescribing. Check current DEA guidance and your state's rules — this is an area where regulations have changed multiple times. When in doubt, consult your medical director.
⚠️ Do not rely on this training for current controlled substance telehealth rules — they change frequently. Always confirm with your medical director or compliance officer before prescribing Schedule II substances via telehealth.
76
The video connection keeps dropping during a patient visit. What do I do?
Telehealth
▼
Answer
Immediate steps: (1) Ask the patient to switch from WiFi to cellular data or vice versa; (2) Both parties try closing and reopening the video link; (3) If video continues to fail, convert to audio-only — call the patient directly and continue the visit by phone; (4) Document in the note: "Video visit initiated; connection unstable. Converted to telephone visit per patient request — audio-only completed." Then determine if the visit qualifies as an audio-only telehealth visit for billing or if it needs to be rescheduled. Don't abandon the patient — complete what care you can.
Ochin Note: Always have the patient's phone number visible before starting a video visit so you can call immediately if the connection fails. Looking up the phone number mid-failure costs precious visit time and frustrates patients.
77
How is a telehealth visit documented differently from an in-person visit in Epic?
Telehealth
▼
Answer
The core SOAP note structure is identical. Additional required elements for telehealth documentation: (1) Visit modality — "This visit was conducted via synchronous audio-video telehealth (MyChart Video Visit)"; (2) Patient location — "Patient located in [city, state] at time of visit"; (3) Consent — "Telehealth consent obtained via MyChart prior to visit" or "Verbal telehealth consent obtained at start of visit"; (4) Physical exam limitations — acknowledge what couldn't be assessed remotely: "Physical exam limited to visual inspection — auscultation and palpation deferred; patient advised to present in person if symptoms worsen." Document what you could assess — general appearance, skin, face, upper extremities visible on camera.
78
Can I see a new patient for the first time via telehealth?
Telehealth
▼
Answer
Generally yes for primary care, with some caveats. CMS and most state Medicaid programs permit new patient evaluations via telehealth (video). However: (1) Your clinic's policy may require an in-person visit first for new patients; (2) Certain visit types (new OB patients, new controlled substance patients) typically require in-person for the initial evaluation; (3) A new patient telehealth visit should include a thorough history, review of any available records via Care Everywhere, and a clear plan for when in-person evaluation is needed. Document that this was a new patient visit and what workup is deferred for in-person follow-up.
79
How is a telehealth no-show handled differently from an in-person no-show?
Telehealth
▼
Answer
Handle it the same way — mark as No-Show in the scheduling disposition. Before marking no-show, make a reasonable effort to reach the patient: call them directly (they may have forgotten or had a technical issue), send a MyChart message, and wait an appropriate grace period (typically 10–15 minutes past the appointment time). Document your contact attempts: "Attempted to call patient at [number] at [time] — no answer. Sent MyChart message. Appointment marked as No-Show after 15-minute wait." This documents due diligence and protects against complaints.
80
What visit types are NOT appropriate for telehealth and should remain in-person?
Telehealth
▼
Answer
Generally NOT appropriate for telehealth: (1) Visits requiring physical examination findings for diagnosis (acute abdominal pain, musculoskeletal injury requiring palpation); (2) Procedures (joint injections, IUD placement, wound care, Pap smears, biopsies); (3) Initial OB visits requiring physical exam and dating ultrasound; (4) New controlled substance evaluations (typically require in-person per policy); (5) Patients in acute distress who need hands-on assessment; (6) Pediatric well visits requiring height/weight measurements and physical development assessment. When in doubt, err toward in-person — telehealth is a supplement to, not a replacement for, in-person care.

84
How does Epic downtime affect my clinic and what should I do?
Ochin-Specific
▼
Answer
Epic has two types of downtime: Planned (scheduled maintenance, usually nights/weekends — announced in advance via my.ochin.org) and Unplanned (system outage). For planned downtime, Ochin provides advance notice and your clinic activates its downtime procedures — paper-based workflows for registration, medication administration, and documentation. For unplanned downtime, Epic's Downtime Viewer (a local cached read-only view of patient records) may be available. Every clinic must have a downtime procedure binder — know where yours is before go-live.
⚠️ Know Your Downtime Procedure Before Day One: "Where is the downtime binder?" is the wrong question to be asking during an active system outage. Your super user and clinic manager will review downtime procedures during go-live training — pay close attention to this session.
85
A patient from another Ochin clinic has information in their chart that looks wrong. Can I correct it?
Ochin-Specific
▼
Answer
It depends on what's wrong. Demographics (address, phone, name spelling): you can update these — they are shared across the network, so your correction benefits all clinics. Clinical information (problem list entry, allergy, medication from another provider): be very cautious. Do not remove or change another provider's clinical entries without their involvement — contact the originating clinic if you believe there is an error. You can always add information (a new allergy, a corrected diagnosis) without removing what another provider documented. Documenting a note of disagreement or clarification via an addendum is also appropriate.
86
What is the Ochin Help Desk and when should I call vs. ask my super user?
Ochin-Specific

87
How does Ochin's shared instance affect patient privacy — can staff at other Ochin clinics see my patients' charts?
Ochin-Specific
▼
Answer
Technically, staff at other Ochin clinics have access to the same shared Epic system. However, all access is logged in an audit trail — every chart view, every update, every click is recorded with the user's name, time, and reason. Staff at another clinic should only access a patient's chart if they have a treatment relationship with that patient. Accessing records without a legitimate reason — even within the Ochin network — is a HIPAA violation and is tracked by Ochin's privacy team. Your patients' records are protected by policy, training, and active monitoring — not by technical walls within the shared instance.
88
What is Population Health in Ochin Epic and how do I use it?
Ochin-Specific
▼
Answer
Population Health tools in Ochin Epic allow care teams to manage panels of patients proactively — not just reactively during visits. Key tools: Reporting Workbench (run lists of patients with overdue preventive care, uncontrolled chronic disease, or care gaps); Care Gap reports (patients due for HbA1c, mammogram, colorectal cancer screening, etc.); Outreach tools (send MyChart messages to a list of patients); and Ochin Analytics dashboards (benchmark your clinic's quality against similar Ochin network sites). Use these to identify and proactively reach out to patients before they become high-acuity — especially before UDS data lock on December 31.
89
How do Epic upgrades work at Ochin and will my workflows change?
Ochin-Specific

Epic releases major upgrades annually (typically in the fall). Ochin manages the upgrade for all 700+ member clinics simultaneously — you don't have to do anything. Check Ochin's release notes or ask your super user if something looks unfamiliar.
90
What is a Super User and what role do they play at my clinic?
Ochin-Specific
▼
Answer
A Super User is a staff member at your clinic who received advanced Epic training before go-live and serves as the first point of contact for workflow questions and real-time support. Super Users: know Epic workflows more deeply than most staff, can troubleshoot common issues, provide elbow-to-elbow support during go-live week, escalate to Ochin Help Desk when needed, and help train new staff after go-live. They are NOT IT staff and cannot change Epic's configuration — but they know how to use the system exceptionally well. Identify your clinic's super users before go-live and don't hesitate to ask them for help.

💊 4.1 The Medication List in Ochin Epic

The Medication List (also called the Medication Reconciliation list) is one of the most clinically critical sections of the Epic chart. It shows every medication the patient is currently taking — prescribed, over-the-counter, vitamins, and herbal supplements. Keeping it accurate is a patient safety imperative.

Accessing the Medication List

  • Patient chart → Medications tab (top toolbar), or
  • Chart navigator → Medications section during a visit note, or
  • Patient header bar → click the medication count link (e.g., "5 Meds")

Medication List Sections

SectionWhat It Shows
Active MedicationsCurrently prescribed and being taken. These appear in every visit note and the AVS.
Patient-Reported MedicationsOTC drugs, supplements, vitamins the patient told the MA/nurse about. Not prescribed here but need to be reconciled.
Historical MedicationsPreviously prescribed medications that have been discontinued. Still visible for reference.
Pending MedicationsOrdered but not yet dispensed or confirmed active. Check if these should be moved to Active.

Medication Reconciliation

At every visit, clinical staff must reconcile the medication list — verifying with the patient that every medication on the list is still being taken as prescribed, and updating the list accordingly. This is a Joint Commission and FQHC quality standard.

  1. Ask the patient to confirm each medication
    Read each medication name and dose aloud. Ask: "Are you still taking this? At the same dose?" Document any discrepancies.
  2. Update changed medications
    If a dose changed or a medication was stopped by another provider: click the medication → Edit to update the dose, or Discontinue if stopped.
  3. Add any new medications not on the list
    Including OTC drugs, supplements, and medications prescribed elsewhere. Click Add Medication → set as Patient-Reported if not prescribed by your clinic.
  4. Click "Mark as Reviewed" or "Reconcile"
    This documents that reconciliation was performed today, updating the "Last Reconciled" date visible to all providers.
⚠️ Reconciliation ≠ Just Reviewing: Simply clicking through the medication screen without asking the patient does not constitute reconciliation. You must verbally confirm each medication with the patient (or their guardian). Unreconciled medications are a leading cause of adverse drug events.

➕ 4.2 Ordering New Medications

Providers order medications directly in Ochin Epic through the Medications activity or from within the visit note's Assessment & Plan section. All outpatient prescriptions flow through Epic's e-prescribing system.

Step-by-Step: Ordering a New Medication

  1. Navigate to Medications tab → click "Order Medication" (or "+ Add")
    Alternatively, within the note editor's Assessment & Plan, type the medication name to launch the order entry.
  2. Search for the medication by name or drug class
    Type the generic name first — Epic displays both brand and generic options. Select the correct formulation (tablet, capsule, liquid, patch, inhaler, injection).

    Example: Type metformin → select metformin 500 mg tablet or metformin 1000 mg tablet
  3. Enter the Order Details
    A structured order form opens. Fill in all required fields (see table below).
  4. Review drug interaction and allergy alerts
    Epic automatically checks the new medication against the patient's allergy list and current medications. Review all alerts before proceeding (see Section 4.4).
  5. Select the pharmacy
    Epic displays the patient's preferred pharmacy. Confirm or change. For controlled substances, additional steps apply (Section 4.5).
  6. Sign the order
    Click Sign (or Sign & Send to transmit to pharmacy immediately). The prescription is sent electronically to the pharmacy via Surescripts.

Dispense as Written (DAW) vs. Generic Substitution

By default, pharmacies substitute generic equivalents unless you mark DAW (Dispense as Written). Only use DAW when there is a clinical reason to require the brand-name drug (e.g., narrow therapeutic index drugs like levothyroxine, or patient stability on a specific formulation). DAW without justification increases patient cost and may trigger payor audits.

Medication Order Sets

Ochin Epic includes pre-built Order Sets for common conditions — groups of medications and orders frequently ordered together. Example: a "Diabetes Initial Management" order set may include Metformin, HbA1c lab, referral to diabetes education, and ophthalmology referral, all placed with one click. Access via: Orders → Order Sets → search by condition.

💡 Tip — Favorite Orders: Add frequently ordered medications to your Favorites list in Epic. From any medication order: click the star icon ⭐ to favorite it. Next time, your favorites appear at the top of search results, saving time every day.

📱 4.3 Electronic Prescribing (eRx) in Ochin Epic

Ochin Epic transmits prescriptions electronically to pharmacies through Surescripts, the national e-prescribing network. This eliminates paper prescriptions (except in limited situations), reduces errors from illegible handwriting, and speeds up the dispensing process for patients.

How eRx Works

  1. Provider signs the prescription in Epic
    Clicking "Sign & Send" initiates an electronic transmission via Surescripts.
  2. The prescription routes to the selected pharmacy
    The patient's preferred pharmacy is pre-loaded from their registration. The provider can change it at order time.
  3. The pharmacy receives it in seconds
    The pharmacy's system receives the prescription electronically, verifies the information, and begins filling — often before the patient leaves the clinic.
  4. Pharmacy can send electronic messages back
    If the pharmacy has questions, needs a prior auth, or has a formulary substitution request, they send an electronic Pharmacy Message that appears in the prescriber's Epic In Basket.

Preferred Pharmacy Setup

The patient's preferred pharmacy is stored in their Epic registration. To view or update:

  • Patient chart → Patient → Pharmacy, or
  • During medication ordering, click the pharmacy field → search for a pharmacy by name, city, or zip code
  • Pharmacies in the Surescripts network are indicated with an ⚡ eRx badge — only these accept electronic prescriptions
  • If a patient uses a mail-order pharmacy, confirm it is in the Surescripts network; some require fax instead

Electronic Prescribing for Controlled Substances (EPCS)

Ochin Epic supports EPCS — electronic prescribing of Schedule II–V controlled substances where permitted by state law. EPCS requires additional identity verification:

  • Providers must complete a one-time EPCS enrollment with Ochin (identity proofing per DEA requirements)
  • Every EPCS prescription requires two-factor authentication at time of signing — your Epic password PLUS a one-time code from the Duo app or a hard token
  • EPCS is logged and audited — all electronically prescribed controlled substances create a tamper-evident audit trail
  • If your state requires paper for Schedule II, Epic will prompt you and generate a printable prescription instead
🔑 Ochin EPCS Status: Ochin has enabled EPCS across its network. Most Ochin member states support e-prescribing for Schedule III–V. Schedule II varies — consult your clinic's pharmacy policy and your state's PDMP/prescribing regulations.

⚠️ 4.4 Drug Alerts & Medication Safety in Epic

Every time you order a medication in Epic, the system automatically runs safety checks in the background and displays alerts if issues are detected. Understanding these alerts — and when to override vs. when to stop — is a critical patient safety skill.

Types of Drug Alerts in Ochin Epic

🚫
HARD STOP — Allergy Alert (Cannot Override)
The medication you ordered belongs to a drug class the patient is allergic to. Example: ordering Amoxicillin for a patient with a documented Penicillin allergy triggers a hard stop. You cannot proceed without removing the order or documenting a clinical override with a valid reason. These are rare but absolute.
⚠️
SOFT STOP — Drug Interaction Warning (Can Override with Reason)
A potential interaction exists between the new medication and something already on the patient's medication list. You can override with a clinical justification. Example: Warfarin + Aspirin — a common combination that requires monitoring but may be clinically appropriate. Read every interaction alert carefully before overriding.
📊
DOSING ALERT — Dose Outside Normal Range
The dose you entered is above or below the standard range for this medication (per patient weight, age, or renal function). Epic checks renal dosing automatically if a recent creatinine/eGFR is in the chart. Always verify dose is appropriate for the patient's current kidney function, especially for Metformin, NSAIDs, and antibiotics.
👶
PEDIATRIC WEIGHT-BASED DOSING ALERT
For pediatric patients, many medications are dosed by weight (mg/kg). Epic calculates the recommended dose range if a current weight is in the flowsheet. If no weight is documented, enter the patient's weight before ordering weight-based medications.

Drug-Drug Interaction Severity Levels

SeverityWhat It MeansTypical Response
■ ContraindicatedCombination should never be used. Serious harm likely.Do not prescribe. Choose an alternative.
■ SeverePotentially life-threatening interaction. Use only if benefit outweighs risk with close monitoring.Consult with pharmacist. Document clinical justification if proceeding.
■ ModerateInteraction may cause adverse effects; monitoring needed.Consider alternatives. If proceeding, counsel patient and plan monitoring.
■ MinorInteraction unlikely to cause significant harm.Acknowledge alert; usually safe to proceed.

Best Practice Advisories (BPAs) — Medication-Related

In addition to drug safety alerts at ordering, Epic's Best Practice Advisories can fire during medication ordering for broader clinical reminders:

  • NSAID + CKD: "Patient has CKD Stage 3 — NSAIDs may worsen renal function. Consider alternatives."
  • Opioid Prescribing: "Patient is on long-term opioid therapy. Consider PDMP check before prescribing." (see Section 4.5)
  • Statin for Diabetic Patients: "Patient has Type 2 DM without a statin on the med list. Consider initiating statin therapy per guidelines."
  • Pregnancy + Teratogenic Drug: "Patient is pregnant. This medication has teratogenic risk (Category D/X). Use with caution or avoid."

Alert Fatigue and Responsible Overriding

Epic presents many alerts — some critical, some routine. Alert fatigue occurs when providers click through alerts without reading them because they appear too frequently. This is a documented patient safety risk.

⚠️ Golden Rule: Read every alert completely before clicking Override or Acknowledge. If you are overriding a drug interaction, you must select a clinically valid override reason from the dropdown — not just "Clinically appropriate" without thought. Override reasons are logged and may be reviewed in audits. Never click through alerts rapidly without reading them.
💡 Tip — Allergy Reconciliation: Alerts are only as good as the data behind them. If a patient says they are NOT actually allergic to Penicillin (it was "sensitivity" or a GI side effect, not a true allergy), update the allergy entry to reflect the correct reaction type. This prevents unnecessary allergy alerts and avoids restricting patients from effective antibiotic options.

🔴 4.5 Controlled Substances & PDMP in Ochin Epic

Controlled substances require additional clinical and regulatory steps in Ochin Epic. Community health centers serve many patients with complex pain, mental health, and addiction histories — safe and responsible controlled substance prescribing is a core FQHC competency.

DEA Schedule Reference

ScheduleExamplesPrescribing Restrictions
Schedule II Oxycodone, Hydrocodone, Morphine, Fentanyl, Adderall (amphetamine), Ritalin (methylphenidate), Cocaine (medical) No refills allowed. New Rx required each month. Many states require EPCS or triplicate paper. DEA 222 form for dispensing.
Schedule III Buprenorphine/naloxone (Suboxone), Testosterone, Codeine combos (Tylenol #3), Ketamine Up to 5 refills in 6 months. EPCS available in most states.
Schedule IV Benzodiazepines (Xanax, Klonopin, Valium), Ambien (zolpidem), Tramadol, Ativan Up to 5 refills in 6 months. EPCS available.
Schedule V Cough syrups with small amounts of codeine (Robitussin AC), Lyrica (pregabalin), Lacosamide Up to 5 refills in 6 months. Some OTC in small quantities in certain states.

PDMP Integration in Ochin Epic

The Prescription Drug Monitoring Program (PDMP) is a state-run database tracking all controlled substance prescriptions dispensed to a patient across all prescribers and pharmacies in the state. Ochin Epic integrates directly with most state PDMPs, allowing providers to check a patient's prescription history without leaving Epic.

  1. When to check the PDMP
    Before prescribing any Schedule II–IV controlled substance. Many states legally require a PDMP check. Ochin's BPA system may automatically prompt you when ordering a controlled substance if a check is overdue.
  2. How to access in Ochin Epic
    From the patient's chart: Chart → PDMP tab, or click the PDMP button that appears in the medication order window when ordering a controlled substance. Epic launches the state PDMP report embedded in the chart.
  3. Review the PDMP report
    Look for: multiple prescribers (doctor shopping), overlapping prescriptions, high morphine milligram equivalents (MME), early refills, and any medications not listed in the Epic med list.
  4. Document your PDMP review
    A PDMP check must be documented in the patient's chart. Ochin Epic has a structured PDMP documentation flowsheet — enter the date of check and findings. Some clinics have a SmartPhrase (.pdmp) for this.
  5. Address any concerns before prescribing
    If the PDMP shows concerning patterns, discuss with the patient before prescribing. Consider consultation with addiction medicine, a pain specialist, or your clinic's behavioral health team.
🟣 Buprenorphine (Suboxone) in Ochin FQHCs: Many Ochin member clinics offer Medication-Assisted Treatment (MAT) for opioid use disorder using buprenorphine/naloxone. Prescribing buprenorphine previously required a DEA X waiver, but as of 2023 federal law eliminated the waiver requirement — any DEA-registered provider may now prescribe buprenorphine for OUD. Ochin has specific workflows for MAT initiation, monitoring, and PDMP documentation. Check with your clinic's MAT coordinator for the local workflow.

Controlled Substance Ordering Workflow in Epic

  1. Order the controlled substance from the Medications activity (same as any medication).
  2. Epic prompts for PDMP check if your state requires it or if your clinic has configured this BPA. Complete the PDMP check in-chart.
  3. Sign with two-factor authentication (EPCS)
    If using EPCS: enter your Epic password + approve the Duo push or enter a one-time code. This satisfies the DEA's two-factor requirement for electronic controlled substance prescriptions.
  4. For Schedule II: no refills
    The refill field will be locked to 0. You must write a new prescription each month. You can post-date a CII prescription up to 90 days in advance in states that allow it (check your state law).
  5. Print if required
    If EPCS is not available for this drug class in your state, print the prescription on tamper-evident paper and sign with wet signature.

Controlled Substance Agreement

For patients on chronic opioid or benzodiazepine therapy, most Ochin clinics require a signed Controlled Substance Agreement (CSA). In Epic:

  • Document the CSA in Chart → Consents or attach a scanned signed copy in Media Manager
  • Ochin has a BPA that fires if a patient is on long-term controlled substances without a current CSA on file
  • CSAs typically require annual renewal and include patient education about risks, safe storage, and naloxone
⚠️ Never pre-sign controlled substance prescriptions or allow non-prescribers to complete and sign controlled substance orders. Every controlled substance prescription must be completed and signed by the licensed prescriber at the time of prescribing. Epic's audit log tracks every action — pre-signing is a DEA violation and grounds for license revocation.

🔄 4.6 Refill Requests & Medication Renewals

Refill management is one of the highest-volume tasks in Ochin Epic. Refill requests come in through multiple channels — from pharmacies electronically, from patients via MyChart, and by phone — all routing to the provider's In Basket.

Sources of Refill Requests

SourceWhere It Appears in EpicHow to Process
Pharmacy (electronic refill request)In Basket → Refill Requests (Rx Renewal)Review, approve or deny electronically. Response sent back to pharmacy automatically.
Patient via MyChartIn Basket → MyChart Messages → Rx Refill RequestReview patient request; process renewal in Medications activity.
Phone call to clinicMA/nurse creates Telephone Encounter → routes to provider In Basket as a refill taskProvider reviews, approves, and sends renewal to pharmacy.
Walk-in / visit requestDuring visit — ordered directly in Medications activityRenew directly during the visit encounter.

Processing a Refill from In Basket

  1. Open In Basket → Refill Requests folder
    Each request shows: patient name, medication, pharmacy, last fill date, and days since last visit.
  2. Review clinical appropriateness before approving
    Check: Is the medication still appropriate? When was the last visit? Are there outstanding labs or monitoring needs? Is this a controlled substance requiring a PDMP check? Is the SFS or insurance coverage current?
  3. Approve: click "Renew" or "Approve Refill"
    A renewal order is created with the same medication, dose, and directions. Review and modify if needed, then sign. The renewal is transmitted electronically to the pharmacy.
  4. Deny with reason: click "Deny"
    Select a reason from the dropdown: "Patient needs to be seen first," "Controlled substance — new Rx required," "Medication discontinued," "Not our patient," etc. A denial message is sent back to the pharmacy electronically.
  5. For borderline cases — create a task for your nurse/MA
    If you need more information (recent labs, updated weight, etc.) before approving, send a task via In Basket to your MA to gather the information and route back to you.

Refill Policies — Common Clinical Guidelines

  • Chronic medications (HTN, diabetes, thyroid): Refillable with 90-day supply if patient has had a visit in the past 12 months and labs are current
  • Controlled substances (CII): No refills — must be seen or assessed; new prescription required each time
  • CIII–CIV controlled substances: May refill up to 5 times in 6 months; still requires clinical assessment at each refill if used chronically
  • Antibiotics, acute meds: Generally not refilled without a new assessment — encourage same-day or virtual visit
  • Inhalers, EpiPens: May refill if clinically appropriate; confirm diagnosis and current need
💡 Tip — Refill Batching: Many providers set aside a specific In Basket check time (e.g., morning and end of day) to batch-process refill requests rather than interrupting patient care throughout the day. Some clinics have MAs pre-screen refill requests and only route complex or controlled substance requests to the provider.
⚠️ Refill without a visit has limits: Refilling chronic medications for patients who have not been seen in over 12 months is a clinical and legal risk. Epic will flag patients with no recent visit. These patients should be contacted to schedule an appointment before further refills are authorized.

🏷️ 4.7 The 340B Drug Pricing Program in Ochin Epic

The 340B Drug Pricing Program (named for Section 340B of the Public Health Service Act) allows eligible healthcare organizations — including FQHCs — to purchase outpatient drugs at significantly reduced prices (up to 50% below commercial cost). This is a major financial benefit for Ochin member clinics and directly supports affordable care for low-income patients.

Why 340B Matters at Your Clinic

  • 340B allows your clinic to buy medications cheaply and dispense or prescribe them to eligible patients, generating revenue that funds clinic operations and patient services
  • Many FQHCs operate an in-house pharmacy or contract pharmacy arrangement to maximize 340B savings
  • 340B savings fund sliding fee discounts, care coordination, behavioral health services, and other mission-critical programs
  • HRSA audits 340B compliance — accurate Epic documentation is essential

340B Patient Eligibility in Ochin Epic

Not every patient or every prescription qualifies for 340B pricing. The drug must be prescribed to a 340B-eligible patient by a provider working at a 340B-registered site for a covered outpatient service.

340B Eligibility CriterionHow Epic Tracks It
Patient is a patient of the FQHC (established relationship)Patient registered in Epic under the 340B-covered organization
Visit is an outpatient encounter at a 340B-registered siteEncounter location in Epic must match a HRSA-registered site
Prescribing provider is employed by or contracted with the covered entityProvider's NPI and employment relationship documented in Epic
Prescription is for an outpatient medication (not inpatient)Encounter type in Epic = Outpatient

How 340B Works in Ochin Epic Prescribing

  1. Provider prescribes as normal
    The medication order and e-prescription process is identical to any other prescription. No additional steps are required at the time of ordering for most 340B workflows.
  2. Epic flags eligible prescriptions automatically
    Based on the patient, encounter type, provider, and site, Ochin Epic can flag prescriptions as 340B eligible in the background. These flags route to the in-house or contract pharmacy.
  3. Pharmacy dispenses from 340B inventory
    The clinic's pharmacy or contract pharmacy purchases the drug at 340B pricing and dispenses it. The billing difference (commercial price charged to insurance minus 340B acquisition cost) is the "340B spread" — revenue to the clinic.
  4. Compliance documentation
    Epic maintains the prescription records needed for 340B audits: encounter date, provider, patient eligibility, drug dispensed, and lot tracking. Do not alter encounter dates or provider attributions — this corrupts 340B compliance data.
🔑 Split-Billing: Some Ochin clinics use split-billing software that integrates with Epic to automatically identify 340B-eligible vs. non-340B prescriptions and manage dual inventories. If your clinic uses split-billing, your pharmacy or billing staff will train you on the specific workflow. As a prescriber, you generally do not need to manually flag 340B — the system does it based on your documentation accuracy.

What Providers Need to Know About 340B Compliance

  • Document the encounter correctly: The visit must be documented as an outpatient encounter at your 340B-registered site. Do not change the encounter location in Epic without clinical reason — it can disqualify 340B eligibility.
  • Do not divert 340B drugs: 340B drugs must be dispensed to eligible patients — never given to staff, samples, or ineligible individuals. Diversion is a federal violation with severe penalties.
  • Telehealth encounters: 340B eligibility for telemedicine visits has specific rules. Check with your compliance officer — the patient's location and encounter type in Epic must be correctly documented.
  • Contract pharmacy: If your clinic uses a contract pharmacy (e.g., a Walgreens or local pharmacy the clinic has a 340B agreement with), prescriptions sent to that pharmacy from eligible patients are handled under the 340B agreement. The pharmacy distinction matters in Epic's routing.
💡 Tip — In-House Pharmacy: If your clinic operates an in-house pharmacy, Epic can display 340B drug pricing directly in the medication ordering screen. When prescribing, you may see a cost comparison: "340B Price: $2.40 / Retail Price: $18.00." This helps providers understand the value being generated for the clinic and encourages directing patients to the in-house pharmacy when clinically appropriate.
⚠️ 340B Audits Are Real: HRSA conducts regular 340B audits. Non-compliance findings can result in repayment demands of hundreds of thousands of dollars and removal from the 340B program — which would devastate a clinic's budget. Every staff member who touches prescribing, billing, or pharmacy must understand their role in maintaining 340B compliance.
